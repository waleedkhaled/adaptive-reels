# Breaking down problems

1. Breaking a problem down means splitting it into small steps that can each be built and checked on their own.
2. A good step has a clear way to verify it is done, such as a test that passes or an output you can inspect.
3. Building the smallest end-to-end version first, even if every part is crude, exposes integration problems early.
4. Writing the steps down frees working memory and makes it obvious what to do next.
5. Listing what you do not know yet turns vague worries into specific questions you can investigate.
6. A short throwaway experiment, often called a spike, answers a risky question before you commit to a design.
7. Estimates for small, concrete steps tend to be more reliable than a single estimate for the whole job.
