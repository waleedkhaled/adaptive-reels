# Encoding and delivery of short videos for instant start

Keep one progressive H.264 MP4 with faststart per reel; HLS/DASH and HEVC/AV1 variants are not worth adding for 30 s clips, and expo-video cannot cache HLS on iOS. Measuring the 119 reels with ffprobe showed three real problems: (1) audio is 317 kbps stereo AAC (Remotion's default) made from 24 kHz mono TTS, which is about 60% of every file, while video is only about 211 kbps; (2) speech sits at about -26 LUFS, roughly 10 LU quieter than the -16 LUFS mobile/podcast target; (3) video is tagged full-range BT.601 (yuvj420p) rather than limited-range BT.709. Fixing audio (mono AAC 64k, loudnorm to -16 LUFS) frees enough bytes to move to 1080x1920 at CRF 20 for about the same 2 to 2.5 MB per reel; this 1080p size is an estimate from upscaled trial encodes, not a real render. Stay at 30 fps, pin High@L4.0, and do not force a 2 s GOP: that is an HLS rule, and keyframes dominate the bitrate on this flat content. Faststart is already correct (33 KB moov at the front, 5 KB first keyframe), so about 100 KB starts playback. Instant start then comes from preloading the next one or two reels with expo-video player instances plus useCaching, immutable cache headers on a range-capable host (Cloudflare R2 with a custom domain fits the free tier), and a native gradient overlay until onFirstFrameRender.

## Recommendations

- **must**: Keep a single progressive MP4 (H.264 + AAC, +faststart) per reel. Do not add HLS/DASH, a bitrate ladder, or HEVC/AV1 variants for the demo. _Why:_ A reel is about 2 MB, twice TikTok's first chunk, and TikTok itself uses whole-file delivery with per-video bitrate choice. HLS adds manifest round trips before the first frame and cannot use expo-video's cache on iOS. With video at 200 to 600 kbps, a newer codec would save well under 1 MB per reel while needing capability detection (AV1 hardware decode only from iPhone 15 Pro; HEVC guaranteed only at Level 3 on Android).
- **must**: Replace the '-c copy' remux in pipeline/src/render/render.ts with an audio re-encode: ffmpeg -y -v error -i video.raw.mp4 -c:v copy -af loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5:LRA=11 -ar 48000 -ac 1 -c:a aac -b:a 64k -movflags +faststart video.mp4 _Why:_ Audio is 317 kbps stereo carrying 24 kHz mono TTS, about 1.15 MB of each 2 MB file, and sits at -26 LUFS. Mono AAC-LC 64k is within Apple's 64 to 128 kbps speech range and cuts audio to about 0.21 MB; loudnorm tested at -16.1 LUFS / -1.5 dBTP. -ar 48000 is required because dynamic loudnorm upsamples to 192 kHz. Use 96k if a listening test finds artefacts. This alone works on the existing files with no re-render (about 1.2 MB per reel).
- **must**: Fix colour at render time in renderMedia: add colorSpace: 'bt709' and imageFormat: 'png' (or keep JPEG with jpegQuality 95+ on Remotion 4.0.532 or later), keep pixelFormat: 'yuv420p'. Verify with ffprobe that the output reports pix_fmt=yuv420p, color_range=tv, color_space=bt709. _Why:_ Current output is full-range BT.601 with missing transfer/primaries tags, which decoders may interpret differently; the dark gradient must match the app's native background and placeholder exactly. PNG frames also remove JPEG ringing around text and code. If you must fix existing files without re-rendering, add to the ffmpeg encode: -vf "scale=in_range=pc:out_range=tv:in_color_matrix=bt601:out_color_matrix=bt709,format=yuv420p" -colorspace bt709 -color_primaries bt709 -color_trc bt709 -color_range tv (command validated locally).
- **must**: Serve reels from content-hashed, immutable URLs (for example reels/<reel_id>.<sha256 prefix>.mp4, the hash already exists in render.json) with Content-Type: video/mp4, Accept-Ranges: bytes, a real Content-Length, and Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable; give the manifest JSON a short max-age. Keep everything on one hostname. Verify each host with: curl -sI <url> and curl -s --range 0-99 <url> -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code} %{size_download}' (expect 206 and 100). _Why:_ iOS requires byte-range support; expo-video's iOS cache breaks on chunked responses without Content-Length; immutable names make re-renders safe to cache forever on device and at the edge. HTTP/2 on one host gives connection reuse on both platforms (expo-video on Android uses OkHttp), so HTTP/3 is not something to chase. Never route MP4s through on-the-fly transformation or compression.
- **must**: Implement prefetch as a small player pool with createVideoPlayer: current reel plus the next one or two candidates, each with source { uri, useCaching: true, contentType: 'progressive' } and bufferOptions.preferredForwardBufferDuration around 5 s while waiting (raise it when the reel becomes active). Have the feed agent decide its next pick one item ahead so there is something to preload, and on release always call player.pause() before player.release(). _Why:_ expo-video fills buffers for players not attached to a view, so the next reel starts without buffering; 5 s is about 0.4 MB per candidate, matching Media3's guidance and far below TikTok's 5 x 1 MB. Preloading two candidates (for example the 'if completed' and 'if skipped' picks) keeps the feed adaptive without a cold start. The pause-before-release step avoids the SDK 57 iOS regression where released players keep playing audio.
- **should**: Move to 1080x1920 at 30 fps using Remotion output scaling (scale: 1.5 on the existing 720x1280 composition) with crf: 20, x264Preset: 'medium', gopSize: 150. If you instead encode with ffmpeg directly, use: -c:v libx264 -preset slow -tune animation -crf 20 -profile:v high -level:v 4.0 -pix_fmt yuv420p -g 150 (leave scene-cut on, no -sc_threshold 0). _Why:_ 720p is upscaled 1.5x or more on current phone screens, which softens text and code; Remotion's own advice for sharper text is output scaling. Estimated 520 to 650 kbps video, so about 2.0 to 2.5 MB per reel after the audio fix, the same as today. Preset medium keeps Level 4.0 automatically; slow or tune animation need -level:v 4.0 or x264 signals Level 5.0. A 5 s GOP cap is enough for progressive playback and replay-from-zero; a 2 s GOP costs roughly 50% more bits on this content. Stay at 30 fps: Android only guarantees 1080p30 H.264 decode on handhelds and 60 fps doubles render time.
- **should**: Host on Cloudflare R2 with a custom domain if you have a domain on Cloudflare (upload with rclone or wrangler r2 object put with content-type and cache-control set). Use the r2.dev URL or the existing pipeline serve.ts on LAN only during development. If there is no domain, use Bunny Storage + CDN (b-cdn.net hostname, $1/month minimum) or the CloudFront Free flat-rate plan. _Why:_ 237 MB and a few GB of demo traffic fit inside R2's free tier (10 GB storage, 10M reads, zero egress), MP4 is cached by default at the edge, and R2 is the service Cloudflare's terms point to for video. r2.dev is rate-limited and uncached. Vercel Hobby (100 MB CLI upload limit, advises against video), Netlify Free (about 15 GB/month) and GitHub Pages (1 GB site, re-renders bloat git history) are worse fits.
- **should**: Do not rely on a video poster prop (expo-video has none). Put an absolutely positioned native overlay above the VideoView that matches frame 0 exactly (the reel background gradient, optionally with the concept title drawn natively) and remove it in onFirstFrameRender. Separately generate one small still per reel for the knowledge map, session lists and review cards: ffmpeg -y -ss 2 -i video.mp4 -frames:v 1 -vf scale=540:960 -q:v 3 poster.jpg (or Remotion renderStill), shown with expo-image. _Why:_ Frame 0 of every reel is just the gradient (5 KB keyframe, elements fade in), so a matching native overlay gives a seamless start with zero extra bytes; a poster taken at 2 s would visibly jump back when playback starts. Android's default SurfaceView shows nothing until the first frame, so the overlay must sit above the video. The local ffmpeg build has no libwebp, hence JPEG. If the new design system wants the hook visible instantly, make the template's frame 0 a complete title card instead of fading in from empty.
- **should**: Add an encode gate to the pipeline (ffprobe per reel) that fails the run unless: codec h264 High, level at most 40, pix_fmt yuv420p, color_range tv, 30 fps, moov before mdat, mono AAC 48 kHz, integrated loudness -16 plus or minus 1 LUFS with true peak at most -1 dBTP, and size at most 3 MB (target 2.0 to 2.5 MB at 1080p). _Why:_ The three defects found here (320k audio, -26 LUFS, full-range BT.601) were invisible because nothing measured them; the repo already has gates and a report, and every check above is one ffprobe or loudnorm print away. It also catches the Level 5.0 trap if someone later adds -tune animation or a slower preset.
- **could**: Before the live demo, warm the device: scroll the scripted demo path once on good Wi-Fi so expo-video's persistent cache holds those reels, then rehearse once in airplane mode to confirm cached playback, and once on cellular to confirm cold-start behaviour. _Why:_ The demo has to prove the loop in about 3 minutes on a real phone, and venue networks are the largest risk to 'instant start'. The cache is persistent and LRU with a 1 GB default, far more than the roughly 16 MB a demo run needs, but offline playback from a fully cached MP4 is not documented and must be confirmed by this rehearsal.

## Fact-check: changes to the recommendations

- Audio command (must-fix): do NOT ship `-af loudnorm=... -ar 48000 -ac 1 -c:a aac`; it clips (+0.9 to +1.5 dBFS measured). Downmix first and leave codec headroom: ffmpeg -y -v error -i video.raw.mp4 -c:v copy -af "pan=mono|c0=0.5*c0+0.5*c1,loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-2:LRA=11:dual_mono=true" -ar 48000 -c:a aac -b:a 64k -movflags +faststart video.mp4 (measured: mono -19.1 LUFS = -16 dual-mono, peak -1.8 dBFS, ~1.0 MB per reel). Drop dual_mono=true only if a listening test wants it 3 dB hotter (-16.6 LUFS mono, peak -1.6).
- Encode gate: state how loudness is measured. A mono file made with dual_mono=true reads about -19 LUFS, so the gate should be -19 +/-1 LUFS on the mono file (or -16 +/-1 measured with dual_mono), plus true peak <= -1 dBTP AND max sample level < 1.0. As written (-16 +/-1 on the mono file) it would pass the loudness of the clipped output and only the peak check would catch it.
- Demo warm-up: raise from 'could' to 'should' and drop the 'not documented' caveat; Expo documents that the cache works offline. Still rehearse in airplane mode because the OS can evict the cache under low storage and the docs say not to rely on it for critical data.
- Hosting headers: remove the 'one hostname / HTTP/2 connection reuse' rationale (expo-video builds a new OkHttpClient per source on Android and a new URLSession per cached asset on iOS). Keep Content-Length and make Content-Type: video/mp4 an explicit must: the iOS cache rejects any MIME type not starting with 'video/' (wrangler/rclone uploads that default to application/octet-stream would break caching on iOS).
- Content-hashed URLs: keep as must, but for a stronger reason. expo-video's iOS cache key is SHA-256 of the URL string and nothing revalidates, so after the planned full re-render (new theme, new audio) the same URLs would serve stale or mixed old/new bytes from device cache. Change the URL (path or query hash) on every re-render, or call clearVideoCacheAsync() on a manifest version bump. Cache-Control headers do not affect the on-device video cache; they only matter for the CDN edge.
- Level pinning rationale: keep High@L4.0, but stop describing Level 5.0 as outside Apple's limit (Apple's MUST is <= High 5.2; 4.1 is a SHOULD for some HLS variants). The real reasons are HLS rule 1.11 (no higher level than needed) and Android decoder headroom.
- Player pool: the SDK 57 release bug is fixed in expo-video 57.0.2+ and SDK 57.0.26 installs ~57.0.5, so treat pause-before-release as insurance rather than a required workaround; install with `npx expo install expo-video` so the pinned ~57.0.5 is used. On Android set surfaceType='textureView' (or avoid contentFit='cover' by matching the 9:16 view) because of the documented overlapping-VideoView known issue in feeds.
- Preload sizing: do not rely on 'about 0.4 MB per candidate' on iOS with useCaching. The cache path proxies AVPlayer's requests through its own URLSession and buffers each response in memory until the request completes or is cancelled, so a preloaded candidate may pull most or all of the file. That is acceptable at 1-2.5 MB per reel, but cap the pool at next 1-2 items and measure real bytes on cellular.
- 1080x1920 upgrade: keep as 'should' only after a real 3-reel render on the actual test phones. The CDD does not guarantee 1080p decode on 720p-panel Android devices and a pool of three 1080p decoders is untested; if the demo Android phone is a 720p-class device, stay at 720p (the audio fix already halves the file).
- Hosting choice for the demo: since development and the demo run in Expo Go against Metro on the laptop, the existing pipeline serve.ts on the same LAN/hotspot (plus a warmed device cache) is sufficient; R2/Bunny is only needed for a build that runs away from the laptop. Cloudflare Pages should not be dismissed on file-size grounds (2 MB files are well under 25 MiB).

## Fact-check: what the track missed

- The recommended ffmpeg audio chain was never measured on its output file; ffmpeg's float stereo->mono downmix after loudnorm adds +3 dB and clips. Any loudness step must be verified by re-measuring the final MP4, not by reading loudnorm's summary.
- Mono vs dual-mono loudness: a mono file reads 3 LU lower under BS.1770 than the same signal in stereo; ffmpeg loudnorm's dual_mono option exists for this. The track's -16 LUFS target and gate ignore it.
- expo-video iOS cache internals relevant to instant start (sdk-57 source): request timeout is hard-coded to 5 s (ResourceLoaderDelegate.requestTimeoutInterval), so on a slow venue network a cached-source request errors after 5 s and the app needs a retry/replace path on player status 'error'; MIME type must be video/*; cache key is SHA-256 of the URL.
- expo-video 57.0.5 (2026-09-24) fixes two iOS cache crashes (data race while trimming the cache; crash when storage is full). These are native fixes, so in Expo Go they depend on the Expo Go binary version installed on the phone, which cannot be pinned from package.json.
- No cross-reel connection reuse in expo-video (new OkHttpClient per source on Android), so every cold reel pays a full TLS handshake; this makes next-item preloading mandatory rather than an optimisation and makes host latency (edge proximity) matter more than HTTP version.
- Expo docs Known issue: overlapping VideoViews with contentFit 'cover' on Android may draw out of bounds (androidx/media issue 1107); workaround is surfaceType 'textureView'. Directly relevant to a vertical paging feed with a preloaded neighbour view.
- SDK 58 is already on npm's `next` tag (expo 58.0.0 published 2026-09-29, 58.0.3 now) while 57.0.26 is `latest`. When SDK 58 goes stable the store version of Expo Go will move to it, and an iPhone cannot easily keep an SDK 57 Expo Go. Plan the demo date around this or have an EAS dev build ready.
- expo-video has on-device generateThumbnailsAsync, an alternative to pre-rendered poster JPEGs for map/session lists (pre-rendered stills are still cheaper at runtime).
- library/reels contains 120 directories but 119 videos: llm-unreliable-dependency--example has only text.json, so the manifest/feed must tolerate or exclude a concept with a missing reel.
- The pipeline's dev server sends Cache-Control: no-cache; this does not stop expo-video's device cache (it ignores HTTP caching headers), so stale reels can persist on the phone during development after re-renders unless URLs change or the cache is cleared.

## Findings

### 1. Audio is about 60% of every reel: 317 kbps stereo 48 kHz AAC wrapped around 24 kHz mono TTS, while video averages only 211 kbps.

Local ffprobe over all 119 files in library/reels: video bitrate avg 211 kbps (min 167, max 274), audio stream 317 kbps AAC-LC stereo 48 kHz, avg duration 29.7 s (23.8 to 37.4), avg size 1.99 MB (1.58 to 2.47), total 237 MB. Source audio parts are MP3 24 kHz mono 55 kbps. pipeline/src/render/render.ts passes audioCodec 'aac' with no audioBitrate; the installed @remotion/renderer 4.0.532 defaults to '320k' (options/audio-bitrate.js, compress-audio.js). Trial encode of the same audio to mono AAC: 64k = 214 KB, 96k = 322 KB per 29 s reel, versus about 1.15 MB now. Remotion docs confirm audio bitrate is configurable via --audio-bitrate / audioBitrate.

Source: <https://www.remotion.dev/docs/encoding> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 2. The reels are about 10 LU too quiet: roughly -26 LUFS integrated against the -16 LKFS (plus or minus 1) target with true peak at or below -1 dBFS that Apple publishes for spoken audio.

Local ffmpeg loudnorm measurement on 6 reels: integrated -25.6 to -26.0 LUFS, true peak -5.7 to -9.6 dBTP, LRA 1.8 to 2.6 LU (consistent between reels, but quiet). Apple Podcasts: 'the overall loudness remains around -16 dB LKFS, with a +/- 1 dB tolerance, and that the true-peak value doesn't exceed -1 dB FS'; mono speech 64 to 128 kbps recommended. Test run of loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5:LRA=11 gave -16.1 / -16.2 LUFS output with TP -1.5 dBTP in single pass (two-pass gave -16.0); both report 'Normalization Type: Dynamic' because +10 dB of gain would push peaks past the ceiling, so the limiter engages (up to about 6 dB on the hottest reel).

Source: <https://podcasters.apple.com/support/893-audio-requirements> · confidence: high

### 3. ffmpeg's loudnorm in dynamic mode upsamples to 192 kHz, so an explicit output sample rate is required; linear mode silently falls back to dynamic when the gain would exceed the true-peak target.

FFmpeg filter docs, loudnorm: 'In dynamic mode, to accurately detect true peaks, the audio stream will be upsampled to 192 kHz. Use the -ar option or aresample filter to explicitly set an output sample rate.' Defaults I=-24, LRA=7, TP=-2. linear: 'the change in integrated loudness shouldn't result in a true peak which exceeds the target TP. If any of these conditions aren't met, normalization mode will revert to dynamic.'

Source: <https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html> · confidence: high

### 4. The current files are full-range BT.601 (ffprobe: pix_fmt yuvj420p, color_range pc, color_space bt470bg, transfer and primaries untagged) even though render.ts asks for yuv420p; Remotion recommends BT.709, and 4.0.532 converts and tags it properly.

Local ffprobe of library/reels/*/video.mp4. Cause: Remotion's default JPEG frames (jpegQuality 80) plus colorSpace 'default' in v4 (DEFAULT_COLOR_SPACE is 'default' unless v5 breaking changes are on). Remotion quality guide: 'Export your video in bt709 for more accurate colors' and switch to PNG frames for better output. In the installed 4.0.532, colorSpace 'bt709' adds zscale=matrix=709:range=limited:primariesin=709:primaries=709:transferin=709:transfer=709 plus -colorspace/-color_primaries/-color_trc bt709 and -color_range tv. A JPEG + bt709 colour bug was reported against 4.0.524 (issue 11440, closed by PR 11331); the installed filter string matches the fixed form. Mis-tagged or full-range video is a known cause of washed-out or shifted colours across decoders (Canva engineering write-up), which would make the reel's first frame mismatch a native placeholder in the same nominal colours.

Source: <https://www.remotion.dev/docs/quality> · confidence: medium · version-sensitive

### 5. TikTok does not use chunk-level adaptive streaming for short videos: each video is fetched as a first 1 MB chunk plus the remainder, bitrate is chosen per video, and the first chunk of the next five videos is always pre-buffered.

Dashlet (NSDI 2023) measurement study: 'the first chunk is the first 1 MB, and the entire remaining video is grouped into a second chunk'; 'TikTok always pre-buffers the first chunk for the next five videos regardless of network conditions'; 'downloads the second chunk when and only when the video starts to play'; 'bitrate adaptation occurring only at video-level (and not chunk-level) granularity'; four options per video (480p, 560p low, 560p high, 720p). Our whole reel is about 2 MB, so a TikTok-sized first chunk would be half the file.

Source: <https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.12954> · confidence: high

### 6. expo-video (SDK 57) preloads by creating a VideoPlayer with a source even when it is not attached to a view, has a persistent 1 GB LRU cache via useCaching that does NOT work for HLS on iOS, and has no poster prop.

Docs v57: 'To preload a video, you have to create a VideoPlayer with a video source. Even when the player is not connected to a VideoView, it will fill the buffers.' 'Due to platform limitations, the cache cannot be used with HLS video sources on iOS.' 'The cache is persistent and will be cleared on a least-recently-used basis once the preferred size is exceeded' (default 1 GB, setVideoCacheSizeAsync). bufferOptions: preferredForwardBufferDuration default Android 20 s, iOS 0 (automatic); minBufferForPlayback 2 s (Android); waitsToMinimizeStalling true (iOS). VideoView has onFirstFrameRender, useExoShutter (Android, default false), surfaceType default 'surfaceView'; no poster prop is documented. contentType accepts 'progressive'. expo-video is included in Expo Go.

Source: <https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/video/> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 7. The 'keyframe every 2 seconds' rule and 6 s segments are HLS authoring rules, not progressive MP4 requirements; Apple's compatibility ceiling for H.264 is High Profile Level 4.1 with frame rates up to 60 fps, and Mux recommends static MP4 for very short assets.

Apple HLS authoring spec: '1.3a. For maximum compatibility, some H.264 variants SHOULD be less than or equal to High Profile, Level 4.1'; '1.4. For H.264, you SHOULD use High Profile in preference to Main or Baseline Profile'; '1.13. Key frames (IDRs) SHOULD be present every two seconds'; '7.5. Target durations SHOULD be 6 seconds'; '1.18 ... Frame rates 23.976, 24, 25, 29.97, 30, 50, 59.94, and 60 fps are supported'; '1.5. The container format for HEVC video MUST be fMP4'. Mux lists static MP4 renditions for 'Supporting assets that are very short in duration (e.g., < 10s) on certain platforms' (https://www.mux.com/docs/guides/enable-static-mp4-renditions). App Review guideline 2.5.7 (the old HLS-over-cellular rule) is now 'Intentionally omitted'.

Source: <https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/http-live-streaming/hls-authoring-specification-for-apple-devices.json> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 8. Android only guarantees H.264 Baseline and Main Profile Level 3.1 (720p30); 1080p decode is mandatory only on displays of at least 1080 lines and only at 30 fps on handhelds; HEVC is guaranteed only at Main Level 3 without a hardware decoder; AV1 hardware decode on iPhone starts at iPhone 15 Pro.

Android 16 CDD 5.3.4: '[C-1-1] MUST support Main Profile Level 3.1 and Baseline Profile'; 'If the height that is reported by the Display.getSupportedModes() method is equal or greater than the video resolution ... [C-2-2] MUST support the HD 1080p video decoding profiles'; table: HD 1080p = 1920 x 1080, '30 fps (60 fps Television)', 20 Mbps. 5.3.5: HEVC '[C-1-1] MUST support the Main Profile Level 3 Main tier'. developer.android.com supported formats: AV1 decoder Android 10+, mandatory as of Android 14. WebKit: 'Safari 17.0 adds support for AV1 video on devices with hardware decoding support, like iPhone 15 Pro and iPhone 15 Pro Max' (https://webkit.org/blog/14445/webkit-features-in-safari-17-0/). High profile is not named in the CDD floor; it is universal in practice but that is not confirmed by a primary source here.

Source: <https://source.android.com/docs/compatibility/16/android-16-cdd> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 9. Level trap: at 1080x1920, x264 with -preset slow and/or -tune animation and no explicit level signals H.264 Level 5.0, outside Apple's 4.1 compatibility ceiling; adding -level:v 4.0 fixes it.

x264 source (common/base.c): tune animation sets i_frame_reference to double (if >1), deblock 1:1, psy_rd 0.4, aq_strength 0.6, bframes +2. Local trial with ffmpeg 8.0.1/libx264 on a reel scaled to 1080x1920: '-preset slow -tune animation' gave level=50; adding '-profile:v high -level:v 4.0' gave level=40 (ffmpeg clips reference frames to the level's DPB). The current 720p files are High@3.1 with ref=3, which is fine. Preset medium (ref=3) stays at Level 4.0 at 1080x1920 without the flag.

Source: <https://code.videolan.org/videolan/x264/-/raw/master/common/base.c> · confidence: high

### 10. On this flat motion-graphics content keyframes dominate the bitrate, so a 2 s GOP costs about 50% more video bits; a 1080x1920 CRF 20 encode is estimated at 520 to 650 kbps.

Local trial encodes of one 28.8 s reel piped to stdout (re-encodes of the existing CRF 23 file, so approximate). 720p: CRF 23 default GOP 250 = 199 kbps; CRF 23 GOP 60 = 312 kbps; CRF 20 GOP 60 = 371; CRF 20 GOP 30 = 525; CRF 20 slow+animation GOP 90 = 332, GOP 250 = 240. Lanczos-upscaled to 1080x1920, CRF 20 slow+animation Level 4.0: GOP 250 = 520 kbps, GOP 150 = 567, GOP 90 = 654; CRF 23 GOP 90 = 503. Existing files already have scene-cut keyframes at 0, 5.4, 13.7 and 21.6 s (keyint=250, scenecut=40). The full proposed command on an existing 720p reel (CRF 20, GOP 150, mono AAC 64k, loudnorm) produced 1.24 MB versus 2.00 MB today. The 1080p figures come from upscaled input, not a true 1080p render.

Source: <file://library/reels/rn-flexbox-layout--example/video.mp4> · confidence: medium

### 11. Faststart is already correct and playback can begin after roughly 40 KB (first frame) to 100 KB (first second); the first frame is an almost empty gradient, and Media3's own guidance is to preload about 5 s of the next item and 3 s of the previous.

Local atom trace: ftyp (32 B), moov (33,251 B) at offset 40, then mdat. First IDR packet is 5,367 B (later keyframes 13 to 49 KB); average luma of frame 0 is 25.5 versus 31.9 at 2 s, and Reel.tsx fades elements in with spring opacity, so frame 0 is essentially the bare background. Total bitrate 537 kbps = 67 KB/s, so 3 s is about 235 KB. FFmpeg docs: faststart 'Run a second pass moving the index (moov atom) to the beginning of the file'. Android Developers blog (Media3 preloading) example: next item 'preload 5 seconds of video' (specifiedRangeLoaded(5000L)), previous item 3 s, distance 2 'just select tracks', up to 4 'prepare' only.

Source: <https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/09/introducing-preloading-with-media3.html> · confidence: high

### 12. The host must answer byte-range requests with 206 and should always send Content-Length; expo-video's iOS cache fails on chunked responses without Content-Length, and on Android expo-video uses OkHttp, so HTTP/2 is the useful protocol level there.

Apple: 'HTTP servers hosting media files for iOS must support byte-range requests, which iOS uses to perform random access in media playback'; test with 'curl --range 0-99 http://example.com/test.mov -o /dev/null'. expo/expo issue 43957 (open, labelled incomplete/outdated): with useCaching: true on iOS, servers using Transfer-Encoding: chunked without Content-Length fail with 'This media may be damaged' (AVFoundationErrorDomain -11849), seen with Cloudflare media transformations. expo-video sdk-57 DataSourceUtils.kt builds an OkHttpDataSource from OkHttpClient.Builder() and wraps it in a CacheDataSource when caching is on; the OkHttp README describes HTTP/2 socket sharing and connection pooling and mentions no HTTP/3. The repo's own dev server (pipeline/src/serve.ts) already implements 206, Content-Range, Accept-Ranges and video/mp4, with Cache-Control: no-cache.

Source: <https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/AppleApplications/Reference/SafariWebContent/CreatingVideoforSafarioniPhone/CreatingVideoforSafarioniPhone.html> · confidence: medium · version-sensitive

### 13. expo-video in SDK 57 had an iOS regression where audio keeps playing after a player is released, which directly affects a preloading player pool; it is closed by PR 47828 and the workaround is to pause before release.

expo/expo issue 47569: in SDK 57, expo-modules-core holds a strong reference to the native object until JavaScript garbage collection instead of freeing it on release(), so the AVPlayer stays alive; reproduced on iOS 18 simulator and an iOS 26.5 device; status closed with PR 47828. Workaround shown: create with createVideoPlayer and call player.pause() then player.release() in cleanup. Released-but-uncollected players also keep their buffered AVPlayerItem in memory. ## Open questions

- Real 1080x1920 bitrate and render time: the 520 to 650 kbps figures come from upscaled re-encodes of existing 720p files, and PNG frames plus scale 1.5 will slow the roughly 1 hour re-render by an unknown factor. Render 3 reels (one code-heavy) and measure before committing.
- Does the expo-video version that SDK 57.0.26 installs include the fix for issue 47569 (PR 47828)? expo-video is not in apps/mobile yet, so this could not be checked.
- Does expo-video's iOS cache play a fully cached progressive MP4 offline, and does it reuse partially buffered ranges from a preloaded player? The docs only say the cache is persistent and LRU.
- How many simultaneous VideoPlayer instances are safe on the target Android phone? The docs give no limit and hardware decoder instance counts vary by device; test a pool of 3.
- Loudness target: -16 LUFS needs up to about 6 dB of peak limiting on the hottest reels (true peak -5.7 dBTP at -26 LUFS). A listening check is needed; -18 LUFS (reported as the AES TD1008 speech figure, but that PDF could not be opened) or raising the level at the TTS stage are alternatives.
- Whether the current full-range BT.601 files actually render with wrong colours on the specific iPhone and Android test devices was not confirmed with a primary source or a device test; compare an old and a re-encoded reel side by side against the native background colour.
- High profile is not part of the Android CDD's guaranteed H.264 floor (Baseline and Main Level 3.1 only). It is used universally in practice, but no primary source was opened that guarantees it; falling back to Main would cost a little efficiency if any test device fails.
- Is a domain on Cloudflare available for an R2 custom domain? Without one, r2.dev is rate-limited and uncached, and Bunny or CloudFront become the better choice.
- Does R2 behind a custom domain always return Content-Length (never chunked) for cached and ranged MP4 responses? Run the curl checks on the real bucket.
