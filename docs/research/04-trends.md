# Mobile design trends and durable practice, 2025-2026

As of October 2026 the two platform languages have converged on the same durable rule and diverged on the fad: Apple (HIG, updated through Sept 2026) and Google (M3 Expressive research) both say chrome must defer to content and emphasis must be spent on one key element, while the "glass everywhere" look of iOS 26 was criticised hard for legibility (NN/g, Oct 2025) and Apple retreated in iOS 27 with stronger diffusion, darkened edges and a user transparency slider. For a dark, full-screen-video learning app the durable set is: solid or lightly blurred tinted-neutral chrome with glass only as progressive enhancement on floating controls, one restrained accent with dark text on accent fills, a two-family type system (legible sans plus a monospace accent for developer data), spring motion split into overshooting "spatial" and non-overshooting "effects" springs, haptics reserved for outcomes, 44 pt targets placed bottom/centre, labelled icons, and full Reduce Motion support. Award winners and respected apps (Tide Guide, Flighty, Linear, Things, Duolingo, Brilliant) get their identity from how they display their own domain data and from a few well-crafted reward moments, not from surface effects; that maps directly to making the knowledge map and the "why this reel" line our signature. Fads to skip: decorative glass/refraction in content, bounce/elastic easing, gradient text and glows, bento-for-its-own-sake, and the generic-AI kit (Inter/Roboto, indigo-purple gradients, three rounded cards, thin-line icons, nested cards, vague copy). Two repo-specific problems surfaced: React Native 0.86 cannot parse oklch()/display-p3 so tokens must ship as sRGB hex, and the current reels bake small text (code at or below about 12 pt equivalent, a chip 52 px from the top, captions 120-150 px from the bottom) exactly where app chrome and the Dynamic Island want to sit, so the re-render should adopt a safe-zone contract. m3.material.io pages could not be opened (JS-rendered), so M3 typography/shape details are lower confidence than the rest.

## Recommendations

- **must**: Design the chrome solid-first and treat Liquid Glass as optional enhancement on at most two floating elements (the tab bar and one mode/quick-action control). Baseline everywhere: a tinted-neutral surface at about 85-92% opacity with a 1 px hairline; on iOS 26+ only, swap to GlassView behind isLiquidGlassAvailable(). Never put glass, blur or refraction behind captions, the 'why this reel' line, retrieval cards or the knowledge map. _Why:_ HIG forbids glass in the content layer and says to use it sparingly; NN/g documented the legibility cost; iOS 27 lets the user change glass transparency system-wide, so the look is out of our control; GlassView is a plain View on Android and iOS below 26; Linear and Things both chose restraint. A solid-first design also looks identical in the 3-minute demo on any phone.
- **must**: Adopt a reel safe-zone contract and re-render to it. On the 720x1280 canvas keep baked text out of the top 100 px and bottom 220 px; remove the baked top chip (top:52) and baked progress bar and draw them natively in the app; play with contain-fit (not cover) and set the screen background to the reel's own end colours (#0B1220 above, #13213D below) so the video reads as edge-to-edge. Put the tab bar in the letterbox band, and the reason line plus quick actions in the reserved bottom 220 px. _Why:_ On a 393x852 pt phone a 9:16 video fitted to width is 699 pt tall, leaving about 153 pt: enough for the status bar inset and a tab bar, nothing more. Today the baked chip sits about 28 pt from the top of the frame (Dynamic Island territory if full-bleed) and captions sit 65-82 pt from the bottom, exactly where overlays would go. Native chrome is also sharper than 720p text upscaled 1.64x.
- **must**: Raise baked text to phone-legible sizes in the re-render: nothing under 24 px on the 720 canvas (about 13 pt), code at 26-28 px with lines wrapped or reformatted to 40 characters or fewer instead of shrinking, diagram labels at 26 px or more, weights Semibold or heavier for anything under 32 px. Evaluate rendering at 1080x1920 if file size allows. _Why:_ Current code text is capped at 22 px (about 12 pt) and falls below Apple's 11 pt minimum for any line over about 48 characters; HIG default body is 17 pt and says avoid light weights. Developers will judge the product on whether code is readable on the phone.
- **must**: Build the colour system as a small OKLCH-authored, hex-shipped token set: one navy-tinted neutral ramp (about 6 surface/line steps sharing the reel's hue, low chroma) plus text primary #F5F7FB and a secondary text no darker than the current #9AA7BD; exactly one interactive accent (sky #7DD3FC) with dark #0B1220 text on accent fills; amber reserved for one meaning only ('due for review'); pink either dropped from UI or given one fixed meaning. Encode mastery states with a single-hue lightness ramp plus an icon/shape per state, never hue alone. Over video use only primary text on a bottom gradient scrim reaching at least 60% black. _Why:_ RN 0.86 cannot parse oklch()/display-p3, so OKLCH is for generating the ramp, not runtime. Computed contrast: white on sky/amber is 1.67:1 while dark navy on them is 11.2:1; muted grey fails over video at any scrim; 60% scrim is the first step that clears 4.5:1 on a white frame. HIG: use brand colour sparingly, don't use one colour for two meanings, don't rely on colour alone. Three decorative accents on dark is also a recognised generic-AI tell.
- **must**: Make every state change legible without motion, and wire Reduce Motion explicitly: keep Reanimated's default ReduceMotion.System, replace spatial transitions with a short opacity change, and ensure the map fill, review resurfacing and answer result are also conveyed by a label/icon change. Provide visible pause/play on the reel (tap to pause) and native captions from the existing word timings rather than relying on audio. _Why:_ With Reduce Motion on, Reanimated jumps straight to end values and skips exiting/shared transitions, so anything communicated only by animation disappears. HIG: motion must not be the only channel; autoplaying video needs start/stop controls. The demo's 'map visibly fills in' beat must survive this setting.
- **must**: Put the product's identity into two things and keep everything else quiet: (1) the knowledge map as a dense, flat, departure-board-style display with mono labels and the single-hue mastery ramp, and (2) the 'why this reel' line written as specific, state-derived copy (for example 'Due: you got useEffect cleanup right 2 days ago' rather than 'Recommended for you'). Run a pre-ship check against the generic-AI list: no indigo/purple gradients or gradient text, no glows or neon shadows, no nested cards or three-equal-card rows, no untinted #000/#FFF, no pulsing dots or chip clutter, no decorative glass, no vague slogans. _Why:_ Award winners and respected apps (Tide Guide, Flighty, Linear, Things) are distinctive through how they show their own data, with restraint around it; the anti-pattern lists from Impeccable and 925Studios agree closely, and a developer audience will recognise the default kit immediately.
- **should**: Use two type families, loaded as static weights with useFonts, and use the same files in the Remotion re-render: one sans for UI and reel headings (400/500/600/700) and one monospace (400/500) used as the developer accent for code tokens, concept IDs, Leitner box numbers, due times and counters with tabular figures. Replace the reel's 'Helvetica Neue, Helvetica, Arial' and 'SF Mono, Menlo' stacks with those files. Minimum sizes in the app: 17 pt body, 13 pt captions/labels, 11 pt absolute floor; support Dynamic Type scaling on text outside the video. _Why:_ Variable fonts are documented as SDK 58+ and iOS only reads named instances; config-plugin embedding does not work in Expo Go. The current stacks resolve differently per render machine and make reels and app look like two products. A sans plus mono pairing is the established look for developer tools (Linear, Geist) and avoids the 'no typographic decision' tell without sacrificing body legibility that HIG asks for.
- **should**: Define six spring tokens in Reanimated and use nothing else: spatial (damping ratio 0.9; stiffness 1400 small, 700 partial-screen, 300 full-screen) for position/size, effects (damping ratio 1.0; stiffness 3800 / 1600 / 800) for opacity/colour. With mass 1 the damping coefficients are about 67 / 48 / 31 and 123 / 80 / 57. No bounce or elastic presets, and no added animation on the reel-to-reel swipe itself. Spend motion on exactly three moments of the demo loop: the reason line changing when the feed adapts, the retrieval result, and changed cells filling in on the knowledge map (animate only cells whose state changed, staggered, when the map opens). _Why:_ The split is Google's research-backed M3 Expressive scheme and prevents overshooting colour/opacity; HIG says avoid motion on frequent interactions and keep feedback brief; Brilliant and Duolingo concentrate animation on progress moments; bounce easing is on every anti-generic list. Coefficients are my conversion from damping ratio and need tuning on device.
- **should**: Use a fixed haptic vocabulary via expo-haptics and nothing outside it: selection on choosing an answer option; notification Success/Error on the retrieval result; impact Light on a quick action ('More like this' / 'Too easy' / 'Not for me'); impact Medium once when a concept crosses into 'learned'. No haptic on swipe or autoplay advance. Add a settings toggle, and always pair with a visual change. _Why:_ HIG: consistent causal mapping, complement other feedback, avoid overuse, make optional, use system patterns for their documented meanings. Haptics silently stop in Low Power Mode, so they can never be the only signal.
- **should**: Navigation and reach: two or three labelled tabs (Feed, Map, optionally You), always visible, navigation only; mode switch (Light/Focus) as a small top control since it is used rarely; the three quick actions as labelled 44 pt targets in a bottom row inside the reserved band with at least 12 pt between them; retrieval answer options as full-width rows in the centre of the screen, minimum 52 pt tall. On the map give exactly one emphasised control (filled accent container, larger) for 'Start focused session' on the selected gap. Use expo-symbols with text labels on every action; no sparkle icons or emoji for the agent. _Why:_ Hoober's field data: centre is most accurate, corners need about 12 mm targets, secondary actions belong on the top/bottom edges; HIG tab-bar and 44 pt rules; M3 research shows one emphasised action is found up to 4x faster and that removing text labels reduces usability. Test NativeTabs in Expo Go first since it is still the 'unstable' import on SDK 57 and its height cannot be changed; fall back to a solid JS tab bar if it misbehaves.

## Fact-check: changes to the recommendations

- Tab bar (recs 1, 2, 9 conflict): do not start with NativeTabs. On iOS 26/27 it always draws Liquid Glass (backgroundColor/blurEffect ignored), its height cannot be measured, and it mounts every tab screen eagerly. That breaks 'solid-first chrome', 'tab bar in the letterbox band' and keeps the video feed mounted behind the Map tab. Use Expo Router JS Tabs with a custom solid tab bar of known height as the primary choice; optionally put a GlassView behind it on iOS 26+.
- Spring tokens (rec 6): write each token as { mass: 1, stiffness, damping } explicitly. Reanimated 4.5.1 (bundled in SDK 57) defaults to mass 4, and dampingRatio cannot be combined with stiffness; leaving mass at default turns the 0.9-ratio tokens into about 0.45 (bouncy).
- Glass gating (rec 1): gate on isGlassEffectAPIAvailable() and isLiquidGlassAvailable() AND AccessibilityInfo.isReduceTransparencyEnabled(), and test on iOS 27 (public since Sept 14, 2026) at both ends of the new Settings > Appearance > Liquid Glass slider. Soften 'glass is a fad to skip': the 2026 ADA citations praise good Liquid Glass integration; the supported position is glass on at most one or two floating controls, never in content.
- Scrim (rec 4): the 60% black scrim and 'primary text only' rule are only needed if overlays can sit on the reel's media region. With the safe-zone contract the reason line and quick actions sit on the reel's own navy (#13213D side), where muted #9AA7BD is 6.6:1 and no scrim is needed. Keep the 60% rule only as a fallback for overlays that overlap bright baked visuals.
- Colour roles (rec 4): amber cannot be 'reserved for due for review' unless the re-render changes the reels, because amber is already baked in as the active spoken-word highlight and Flow arrows. Decide in the re-render: either move the caption/flow highlight to another token or pick a different 'due' colour. Pink needs no migration (unused in the Remotion components).
- Type (rec 5): do not apply the web 'no system fonts' rule to native UI. HIG Branding (Sept 9, 2026) endorses a custom font for headlines with system fonts for body/captions. Cheapest durable set: one OFL display/sans for headings shared with the Remotion render, one OFL mono, system font for body. Static weights via useFonts is right for SDK 57; revisit once SDK 58 is stable (variable fonts).
- Safe zone (rec 2): compute bands at runtime from useSafeAreaInsets and window size instead of hard-coding the 393x852 case; iOS 27 makes iPhone apps resizable and HIG added 'Designing for iPhone Duo' (Sept 9, 2026). Contain-fit is also the expo-video default and avoids a documented Android bug where overlapping VideoViews with contentFit 'cover' draw out of bounds (workaround surfaceType 'textureView'). HIG Layout says full-screen background content should extend under tab bars, which supports painting the screen with the reel's end colours.
- Touch targets (rec 9): use 48 as the floor for quick actions and tab items so one size satisfies both Apple (44 pt) and Android (48 dp).
- Haptics (rec 8): map the vocabulary per platform; on Android use performAndroidHapticsAsync types instead of impactAsync, per the Expo doc.
- Open question 1 is closed for native tabs (documented as included in Expo Go); the zoom transition remains alpha with a 1 s delay and should stay out of the demo.

## Fact-check: what the track missed

- Expo Go deadline: SDK 58 beta started Sept 15, 2026 with a 3-4 week beta, and Expo states the App Store/Play Expo Go 'will update to SDK 58 shortly after the stable release' and drop SDK 57. Around mid-October the SDK 57 project may stop opening in store Expo Go on real iPhones. Plan for either upgrading to SDK 58 (RN 0.88, built for iOS 27) or an `eas go`/dev build before the demo. Source: https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58-beta
- Since early Sept 2026, Expo Go on a physical iPhone opens a dev-server project only when Expo CLI and Expo Go are signed in to the same Expo account; a demo-day gotcha on a second phone. Source: https://docs.expo.dev/get-started/set-up-your-environment/
- The track never answered the user's actual dashboard question (heatmap vs knowledge graph vs hybrid) with evidence; 'departure-board style' is an analogy from Flighty, not a comparison. Nothing was checked on node-link legibility for 40 nodes on a phone, or on rendering options that work in Expo Go (SDK 57 bundles @shopify/react-native-skia 2.6.2 and react-native-svg 15.15.4).
- Android is almost absent: 48 dp targets, edge-to-edge and gesture-bar insets, taller aspect ratios (more letterbox), Android haptics API, Material bottom-bar behaviour. The safe-zone numbers were derived for one iPhone size only.
- Baked reel text cannot follow Dynamic Type or the 200% enlargement guidance; native captions (expo-video exposes subtitle tracks) are the accessible path and should be part of the re-render contract (ship a caption track per reel, not only burned-in words).
- isLiquidGlassAvailable() also depends on build SDK and the UIDesignRequiresCompatibility Info.plist key; SDK 58 notes a fix so it reports true when built with the iOS 27 SDK, which suggests an SDK 57 build made with Xcode 27 may report false. Unverified inference; test before relying on glass in an EAS build.
- The mobile app is still a bare scaffold (expo ~57.0.26, react-native 0.86.3, no router/reanimated/video installed), so every library recommendation is untested in this repo. File: apps/mobile/package.json
- The generic-AI checklist sources are web-focused opinion pieces (one published today); treat them as a lint list, not as evidence about mobile app quality.

## Findings

### 1. Apple's HIG restricts Liquid Glass to the floating controls/navigation layer, forbids it in the content layer, and gives a specific rule for controls over video: use the 'clear' variant only over visually rich media and add a 35% dark dimming layer when the underlying content is bright.

HIG Materials (change log: Liquid Glass guidance added June 9, 2025, updated Sept 9, 2025): "Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer." "Use Liquid Glass effects sparingly... Limit these effects to the most important functional elements in your app." "Only use clear Liquid Glass for components that appear over visually rich backgrounds." "If the underlying content is bright, consider adding a dark dimming layer of 35% opacity." Regular variant is for components with significant text. HIG Color adds: "Apply color sparingly to the Liquid Glass material... Refrain from adding color to the background of multiple controls."

Source: <https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials.json> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 2. Liquid Glass as shipped in iOS 26 was judged a legibility and predictability regression by NN/g, and Apple partly retreated in iOS 27 (announced June 2026): more diffusion, darkened edges, a uniform toolbar backing, and a system-wide user transparency slider from 'ultra clear' to 'fully tinted'. An app therefore cannot assume how its glass will look.

NN/g, Raluca Budiu, Oct 10, 2025: transparency makes text-on-text "an illegible mess"; tab bars are squeezed, targets smaller and crowded, controls appear/disappear; "Motion for motion's sake is not usability." MacRumors, June 10, 2026 (https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/10/how-liquid-glass-is-changing-in-ios-27/): Apple "adjusted Liquid Glass so it more effectively diffuses complex content", added "a darkened edge around Liquid Glass elements, along with brighter specular highlights", a "transparency slider in Settings", and a uniform toolbar when content scrolls under bars; changes apply automatically without recompiling. Apple's HIG 'What's new' lists June 8, 2026 updates to scroll edge effects, app icons and tab bars and 'Reintroduced design principles' (https://developer.apple.com/design/whats-new/).

Source: <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 3. The best-regarded developer-audience app, Linear, deliberately did NOT adopt Apple's Liquid Glass API on mobile: it built its own frosted material (blur + gradient + specular highlight) and dropped refraction for legibility; Things adopted glass only as 'a touch' in specific places.

Linear, Oct 21, 2025: Apple's APIs "wouldn't give us the control we needed"; Liquid Glass was "a moving target throughout the beta period"; "The one effect we chose not to reproduce was Liquid Glass's refraction" because it "can make dense professional interfaces harder to read"; philosophy "purpose-built, disciplined, and designed for sustained focus"; material adapts to Increase Contrast with solid outlines; navigation is a customizable bottom toolbar. Things 3.22 (Sept 15, 2025, https://culturedcode.com/things/blog/2025/09/things-for-os-26/): "a touch of glass in the sidebar", "wider spacing that feels a bit more relaxed", buttons that respond "with a subtle glow and scale".

Source: <https://linear.app/now/linear-liquid-glass> · confidence: high

### 4. In Expo SDK 57, expo-glass-effect is in Expo Go but renders real glass only on iOS 26+ (plain View elsewhere, including Android); native tabs are still under the 'unstable' import in SDK 55-57 and their height and iOS 26 background cannot be customised; the zoom (shared-element-like) transition is alpha, iOS 18+ only, with a documented ~1 s delay.

Expo v57 GlassEffect doc: included in Expo Go; "GlassView is only available on iOS 26 and above. It will fallback to regular View on unsupported platforms"; styles 'clear' | 'regular' | 'none'; isLiquidGlassAvailable(); "Setting opacity to 0 on GlassView or any of its parent views causes the glass effect to not render at all." Native tabs doc (https://docs.expo.dev/router/advanced/native-tabs/): SDK 55-57 import from 'expo-router/unstable-native-tabs' (stable path is SDK 58+); on iOS 26+ the bar "derives its background from the content behind it" and backgroundColor/blurEffect only apply on iOS 18 and earlier; Android uses Material bottom navigation, max 5 tabs; height not customisable. Zoom transition doc (https://docs.expo.dev/router/advanced/zoom-transition/): alpha, iOS only, users "may experience a noticeable delay (approximately 1 second)". Neither doc states whether native tabs or zoom transitions work in Expo Go.

Source: <https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/glass-effect/> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 5. Material 3 Expressive's research-backed core is 'make the one key action obvious through colour, shape, size, motion and containment', with an explicit caveat that breaking familiar patterns or removing text labels hurts usability; its motion system replaces durations with two spring families: spatial (slight overshoot) and effects (no overshoot).

Google Design: 46 studies, 18,000+ participants; "Participants were able to spot key UI elements up to four times faster"; older users matched younger ones across 10 apps; a scattered playlist redesign "looked modern" but "usability scores suffered"; removing text labels from actions "resulted in decreased usability". Material Components Android Motion.md (https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md): spatial springs damping 0.9 with stiffness 1400 (fast, small components) / 700 (default, partial-screen) / 300 (slow, full-screen); effects springs damping 1.0 with stiffness 3800 / 1600 / 800; "Effects springs are used to animate properties such as color or opacity where the property's value should not be overshot." Compose Material3 1.4.0 is the latest stable (Sept 23, 2026) and several expressive component APIs were promoted to stable in 1.5 alphas (https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/compose-material3). m3.material.io pages (emphasized type styles, 35 shapes, I/O 2026 layout system) could not be rendered and are known only from search snippets.

Source: <https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research> · confidence: medium · version-sensitive

### 6. Apple's motion, haptics and accessibility guidance is stable and specific: no decorative motion on frequent interactions, animations must be cancellable, haptics must have a consistent causal meaning and be reserved for discrete events, Reduce Motion means tighter springs and fades instead of spatial transitions, and autoplaying video needs start/stop controls. Reanimated 4 honours the OS setting by default by jumping to end values.

HIG Motion: "Don't add motion for the sake of adding motion"; "generally avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently"; "don't make people wait for an animation to complete". HIG Playing haptics (https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics.json): "build a clear, causal relationship between each haptic and the action that causes it"; "Avoid overusing haptics"; notification haptics (success/warning/error) are for task outcomes, selection for changing values, impact for collisions/snaps. HIG Accessibility (https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility.json): under Reduce Motion tighten springs, replace x/y/z transitions with fades, avoid blur animations; "Avoid autoplaying audio and video content without also providing controls to start and stop it." Reanimated 4.x docs (https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-reanimated/docs/guides/accessibility/): default ReduceMotion.System; withSpring/withTiming "return the toValue immediately"; layout animations reach endpoints instantly. expo-haptics v57 doc: haptics do not fire in Low Power Mode or when disabled by the user.

Source: <https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion.json> · confidence: high

### 7. Apple's durable type rules: 17 pt default and 11 pt minimum on iOS, avoid Ultralight/Thin/Light, support Dynamic Type up to at least 200%; and its current branding advice is a custom font for headlines with system fonts for body, brand colour used sparingly on controls and moved into the content layer.

HIG Typography: iOS default 17 pt, minimum 11 pt; "prefer Regular, Medium, Semibold, or Bold font weights, and avoid Ultralight, Thin, and Light"; emphasized weights added to Dynamic Type specs Dec 16, 2025. HIG Branding, refined Sept 9, 2026 (https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/branding.json): "It can work well to use a custom font for headlines and subheadings while using system fonts for body copy and captions"; "Apply your app's accent color judiciously"; "consider moving it into the content layer, where it scrolls beneath Liquid Glass controls"; "Ensure branding always defers to content." Developer-audience precedent: Linear uses Inter Display for headings and Inter for text (https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui); Vercel's Geist Sans/Mono/Pixel is OFL and "specifically designed for developers and designers" (https://vercel.com/font).

Source: <https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography.json> · confidence: high

### 8. On SDK 57 in Expo Go, custom fonts must be loaded at runtime with useFonts and should be static-weight files: Expo documents variable-font support as SDK 58+, and on iOS only a variable font's named instances are reachable. Separately, the current reels use a machine-dependent font stack and bake text that is at or below Apple's minimum size when shown on a phone.

Expo fonts guide: variable fonts "supported in SDK 58+"; Android reads the wght axis (Android 10+), "iOS reads the named instances the file declares"; the config-plugin embedding "doesn't work with Expo Go"; useFonts "works with all Expo SDK versions and with Expo Go"; OTF/TTF supported. Repo (read-only): pipeline/src/render/remotion/theme.ts sets font to '"Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif'; Code.tsx uses '"SF Mono", Menlo, Consolas, monospace' at Math.min(22, floor(588/(longest*0.6))) px; Flow.tsx labels 22 px; Reel.tsx top chip 26 px at top:52, captions at bottom:120-150, baked 8 px progress bar at top:0. A 720 px canvas shown 393 pt wide is 0.546 pt per px, so 22 px is about 12 pt and any code line over about 48 characters drops under 11 pt; the 720 px frame is also upscaled about 1.64x on a 3x screen, so baked text is visibly softer than native UI text.

Source: <https://docs.expo.dev/develop/user-interface/fonts/> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 9. React Native's colour parser accepts only hex, rgb(a), hsl(a), hwb and named colours, so OKLCH and Display-P3 are usable only as an authoring method, not as runtime values; the practice worth copying is Linear's: generate the whole theme in a perceptually uniform space from three inputs (base, accent, contrast) and keep chroma in neutrals low.

facebook/react-native packages/normalize-color/index.js (main) defines matchers for rgb, rgba, hsl, hsla, hwb and hex only; reactnative.dev/docs/colors lists the same plus PlatformColor/DynamicColorIOS and no color()/oklch (https://reactnative.dev/docs/colors). Linear (Mar 28, 2024, https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui): LCH is perceptually uniform so equal lightness looks equal across hues; themes reduced from 98 variables to base, accent, contrast, which also yields high-contrast themes; a "more neutral and timeless appearance" came from limiting how much blue chroma went into neutrals. Evil Martians (https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/oklch-in-css-why-quit-rgb-hsl): OKLCH gives predictable lightness/contrast and palette generation, fixes LCH's blue hue shift, but out-of-gamut values get clipped. Impeccable README: "Don't use pure black/gray (always tint)".

Source: <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebook/react-native/main/packages/normalize-color/index.js> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 10. Text over imagery needs a worst-case-tested scrim, and Apple's 35% dim is not enough for plain text: computed against the current reel palette, white text over a 35% black scrim on a white frame is only 2.3:1, a 60% scrim reaches 5.4:1, and the muted grey #9AA7BD fails over video at any practical scrim. On the reel's own navy background the palette is comfortably accessible.

NN/g 'Text over images' (2015, last reviewed Jan 13, 2026): require 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text); use "a semiopaque overlay..., a blur, a text shadow or outline, or a combination"; "provide a high enough contrast for the worst-case background image and text placement". Computed WCAG ratios (my calculation): on #0B1220 text #F5F7FB 17.5:1, muted #9AA7BD 7.7:1, sky #7DD3FC 11.2:1, amber #FBBF24 11.2:1, pink #F472B6 7.1:1; on #13213D muted is 6.6:1. White on a sky or amber fill is 1.67:1 (fails) while #0B1220 on those fills is 11.2:1. Scrim over a white frame: 35% gives 2.27:1 for #F5F7FB, 50% gives 3.68:1, 60% gives 5.35:1, 70% gives 7.88:1; muted grey reaches only 3.5:1 even at 70%.

Source: <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-over-images/> · confidence: high

### 11. The popular 'thumb zone' chart is not supported by its own author's field data: people shift grip constantly and are most accurate at the screen centre, so the durable rule is primary content in the centre, secondary actions along the top and bottom edges with larger targets toward corners. Apple's numbers are 44x44 pt default, 28 pt absolute minimum, and 12-24 pt spacing; its tab-bar rules are navigation only, always visible, labelled, few tabs.

Hoober, UXmatters, March 2017: "people prefer to view and touch the center of the screen"; targets "as small as 7 millimeters" at centre, "corner target sizes must be about 12 millimeters"; the "well-known, but incorrect thumb-sweep chart"; "always place the primary content at the center of the screen" and "place secondary actions along the top and bottom edges"; 75% tap with one thumb, fewer than 50% hold one-handed. HIG Accessibility: iOS controls 44x44 pt default, 28x28 minimum; about 12 pt padding around bezelled elements, 24 pt around unbezelled. HIG Tab bars (updated June 8, 2026, https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars.json): "Use a tab bar to support navigation, not to provide actions"; "If you hide the tab bar, people can forget which area of the app they're in"; include labels; "prefer a monochromatic appearance for tab bars" over colourful content.

Source: <https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2017/03/design-for-fingers-touch-and-people-part-1.php> · confidence: high

## Open questions

- Do expo-router native tabs (unstable import on SDK 57) and the alpha zoom transition actually run in Expo Go on the test phones? The docs I opened do not say; the zoom transition also documents a ~1 s delay, so it should not be relied on for the demo without a device test.
- Expo's docs reference SDK 58 (stable native-tabs import, variable-font support). I did not verify whether SDK 58 is released or still pre-release as of 2026-10-04; if it is stable, the font and tab recommendations loosen.
- Which exact sans and mono files to standardise on (for example Geist Sans/Mono, or system SF plus JetBrains Mono)? I confirmed Geist's licence (OFL) and intent but did not verify an @expo-google-fonts package or that Remotion renders the same files identically.
- Is re-rendering at 1080x1920 affordable in file size and decode cost on the target phones? I only established that 720p text is upscaled about 1.64x on a 3x screen, not the size/performance trade-off.
- How do GlassView and the native tab bar respond to the iOS 27 user transparency slider and to Reduce Transparency inside a React Native app? Apple says improvements apply automatically, but I found no RN-specific confirmation.
- Small or 16:9 screens (iPhone SE class) have no letterbox band and tall Android phones have more; the safe-zone numbers (top 100 px, bottom 220 px) are derived from a 393x852 pt layout and need checking on the real test devices and against Apple's new 'Designing for iPhone Duo' page (HIG, Sept 9, 2026), which I did not read.
- Should the app ship a light theme at all? HIG says to supply light and dark colour variants even for single-appearance apps so Liquid Glass can adapt; for a dark-video demo I assumed dark-only.
- Material 3 Expressive details beyond the research article and the Android motion tokens (emphasized type styles, the 35-shape library, the I/O 2026 Expressive layout and spacing system, Android 17 blur) come from search snippets only because m3.material.io would not render; treat them as unverified.
- I did not examine Arc Search, Bear or Imprint through primary sources, and Duolingo's art-style post is from 2020; conclusions about learning-app visual style rest mainly on Duolingo, Brilliant and the award citations.
- React Native wide-gamut support: the docs and JS colour parser show no color(display-p3) syntax, but an iOS-level default colour space option may exist (PR #42830, not opened). Not needed for the demo, unresolved for later.
