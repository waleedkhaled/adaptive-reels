# Visualising a 40-node prerequisite graph on a phone

Build a hybrid, not a free-form knowledge graph: a 5 x 8 tile grid (the planned "heatmap", topic rows, concepts ordered by prerequisite depth) as the always-visible overview, with graph edges drawn only for the selected concept and a half-height bottom sheet for detail and the "start gap session" action. The repo's own graph decides this: 35 of 38 prerequisite edges stay inside a topic, but the widest global layer has 20 nodes, which gives a 17.9 pt pitch on a 390 pt screen (below every touch-target guideline), while 8 tiles per row land at 44.75 pt, almost exactly Apple's 44 pt default. Research backs the split: node-link diagrams lose to alternatives above about 20 nodes except for path finding, radial trees are slower than top-down ones, panning was the slowest technique in the mobile study Cockburn et al. review, and Duolingo dropped its tree for a path because learners were unsure what to do next. Colour cannot carry the five ordered states alone: on the #0B1220 to #13213D background at most two lightness levels can be mutually 3:1, sky and amber have identical luminance (1.00:1), and pink collapses into the muted grey under deuteranopia, so state needs fill level or glyphs with a single-hue ramp as reinforcement. For the "map fills in" demo beat, replay changes when the map opens, staged and at roughly 450-600 ms per tile, with a Reduce Motion fallback because Reanimated skips animations by default when that setting is on. No primary study of node-link DAGs on phone-sized touchscreens was found, so the phone-specific layout argument rests on geometry plus desktop graph-readability and mobile small-multiples studies.

## Recommendations

- **must**: Make the knowledge map a hybrid: a 5 x 8 tile grid as the default view (one row per topic with the topic name above it, 8 concept tiles per row ordered left to right by prerequisite depth, then topological order), with no edges drawn at rest. Use a 44.75 pt hit pitch on 390 pt (16 pt side margins) with a visible tile of about 38-40 pt. Below about 380 pt/dp width, wrap each topic to 2 rows of 4. _Why:_ It is the only layout that shows all 40 concepts at guideline touch size on one screen without panning (global layered DAG: 17.9 pt pitch; panning was slowest on mobile in the Cockburn review). Ordering by depth keeps graph meaning in position. A treemap or hex grid adds nothing here because all concepts have equal weight and adjacency carries no meaning. On a 360 dp Android phone 8 columns give 41 dp, under Material's 48 dp, hence the wrap fallback.
- **must**: Show graph structure on focus only: tapping a tile selects it, draws just its direct prerequisite and unlock edges as solid arrows in a react-native-svg overlay, highlights the full ancestor chain and descendants, and dims every other tile to about 40% opacity. Show RELATED_TO as named chips in the detail sheet first, and only add dashed lines if a prototype shows they stay readable. _Why:_ Selection involves a mean of 2.45 edges (max 7) instead of 60, so no bundling or crossing minimisation is needed. Node-link's one consistent strength is path finding, which is exactly the 'what do I need before this' question. 18 of 22 related edges cross topic rows and would draw long diagonals over other tiles.
- **must**: Put detail in a bottom sheet at the medium (about 50%) detent: concept title, state in words, box and next due time, the prerequisite chain as a vertical rail list (rows at least 44 pt, full titles), related chips, and one primary button to start the gap session. Use Expo Router presentation 'formSheet' with sheetAllowedDetents [0.5, 1] and sheetLargestUndimmedDetentIndex 0 so the grid stays visible and tappable. Tap is the only required gesture; no long-press or pinch. _Why:_ Titles (median 16, max 36 characters) cannot be labelled on 44 pt tiles at Apple's 11 pt minimum text size, so labels must come on demand. The grid plus labels needs about 320-340 pt and fits above a 422 pt sheet. A vertical rail gives every prerequisite its own legible row, the phone-friendly form of a 'metro/path' layout. Apple advises against custom multi-finger gestures and against hidden gestures as the only route.
- **must**: Encode the five ordered states by fill level first and lightness second, in one hue: unseen = empty tile with a 1.5 pt slate-500 #64748B outline; seen = outline plus small centre dot; checked = bottom half filled sky-500 #0EA5E9; learned = fully filled sky-300 #7DD3FC; retained = fully filled sky-100 #E0F2FE with a dark check glyph. Due = amber #FBBF24 corner dot with a 2 pt background-colour halo plus a clock glyph in the sheet. Locked = lock glyph and dashed outline, still tappable to explain which prerequisite is missing. If box 3 and box 4 must differ, use one versus two check marks, not a sixth colour. _Why:_ At most two lightness levels can be mutually 3:1 on this background and adjacent ramp steps are only 1.45-1.7:1 apart, so shape must carry the order (WCAG 1.4.1, G111). All listed colours are at least 3.36:1 against both ends of the gradient. Sky and amber have identical luminance, so 'due' needs its own shape and position. Lightest = most follows dark-theme convention, and five steps sits inside ColorBrewer's 5-7.
- **must**: Stage the 'map fills in' beat as a replay when the map opens (and live if it is already visible): stage 1, 150-200 ms, dim unchanged tiles to about 40%; stage 2, each changed tile fills over 450-600 ms with standard easing cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1) and one scale pulse to about 1.15, staggered about 100 ms, animating tiles in the same row together when more than 5 changed; stage 3, 300 ms, un-dim and tick a header counter ('Learned 3 of 40' to '4 of 40'). Keep the whole sequence under about 2.5 s and never loop; limit any 'due' pulse to 2 cycles. _Why:_ The state change happens in the feed, away from the map, which is the textbook change-blindness case; replaying one change at a time with the rest dimmed is the documented remedy. Heer and Robertson found animation beats static and simple staging is preferred, with about 1 s per stage for large movement and less for minimal movement; NN/g and Material put small-element motion at 100-600 ms. Persist a 'last seen map state' snapshot to drive the replay. WCAG 2.2.2 caps unattended motion at 5 s.
- **must**: Build tiles as ordinary React Native Pressables in a flex grid, with a single non-interactive SVG overlay (pointerEvents none, hidden from accessibility) for focus edges; do not render the map in a Skia canvas. Give each tile accessibilityRole 'button', accessibilityLabel '{concept}, {topic}', accessibilityValue.text such as 'learned, due for review' or 'locked, needs {prerequisite}', accessibilityState.selected, and each topic header role 'header' with a summary like 'React Native, 3 of 8 learned, 1 due'. _Why:_ 40 views are trivial for performance; real views give VoiceOver and TalkBack a per-concept structure for free, whereas Skia needs a parallel layer of overlay views. Both libraries ship in Expo Go, so this is a simplicity and accessibility choice, not a compatibility one. The topic-then-concept order gives the hierarchical structure screen-reader users need.
- **should**: Add an explicit Reduce Motion path: no scale or stagger; changed tiles swap state with a short opacity cross-fade marked reduceMotion: ReduceMotion.Never (or an instant swap plus a 1 s highlight outline), the header counter still updates, and call AccessibilityInfo.announceForAccessibility with text such as 'Flexbox layout is now learned'. _Why:_ Reanimated's default ReduceMotion.System makes timing and spring animations jump to their end value, so without this the key demo beat silently disappears on a phone with Reduce Motion enabled. Apple's guidance is to replace movement with fades, not to remove feedback.
- **should**: Offer a Map | List segmented control; the list is a SectionList grouped by topic with each concept's state in words, sorted due first, then gaps whose prerequisites are met, then locked, and each row opening the same sheet. _Why:_ It is the long-description equivalent that WAI asks to be available to everyone, it scales with Dynamic Type where a 44 pt tile cannot, and it is the natural fallback for narrow Android screens. It reuses the sheet and state-label code, so the cost is small.
- **should**: Do not build a free-form node-link canvas, constellation, radial or sunburst view, or pinch-zoom for the demo. If a visible 'graph' is wanted later, make it a per-topic view (8 nodes, at most 6 per layer, 59.7 pt pitch on 390 pt, depth running top to bottom) with coordinates precomputed at content-build time from longest-path layers; d3-dag 1.1.0 (sugiyama, zherebko, grid layouts, no DOM dependency) is available if hand layout becomes tedious. _Why:_ Radial layouts tested slower than top-down ones; node-link loses above about 20 nodes on most tasks; free pan and zoom costs reorientation; Duolingo's tree confused learners about what to do next. Per-topic sub-graphs are small enough to be legible, and 35 of 38 prerequisite edges stay inside a topic, so little is lost by splitting.
- **could**: If the reels are re-rendered with a new theme, keep sky and amber as the only two semantic hues (progress and due), keep pink for decoration only and never as a state next to grey, and give the four feed-pool colours an icon or label as well. Record in the design tokens that sky and amber are equal in luminance. _Why:_ Blue versus yellow survives red-green colour blindness (simulated delta-E about 104-107), while pink versus muted grey collapses (delta-E 7 under deuteranopia) and sky versus pink shrinks to about 24-27. Equal luminance means sky versus amber is invisible in greyscale or to anyone relying on contrast, so every such distinction needs a second cue.

## Fact-check: changes to the recommendations

- Detail sheet (rec 3): do not build it as an Expo Router formSheet at a 0.5 detent for the demo. On iOS 26 a partial-height formSheet is a floating Liquid Glass sheet with touch flares that contentStyle cannot cover (react-native-screens #4605, open, affects the 4.26 line SDK 57 pins) and Expo Go cannot be patched. Use an in-screen bottom panel (plain View animated with Reanimated) on the map screen; it also removes the route/param plumbing for 'tap another tile while the sheet is open'. If formSheet is kept anyway, test on the actual phone first and remember the default dims and blocks the background.
- Sheet height (rec 3): 50% covers the bottom topic row once the status bar, header, Map|List control and counter are counted (about 505-545 pt needed vs about 450 pt available on 390x844). Use a panel of about 35% height, auto-scroll the selected row above it, and put the Map|List control in the header.
- Focus edges (rec 2): replace 'solid arrows in an SVG overlay' with either arcs routed through a reserved 20-24 pt gutter above each row, or no lines at all (ring/badge prerequisites vs unlocks, plus the rail list in the panel). 28 of 35 intra-topic edges run along the row through other tiles, and one node has 6 dependents in its own row. Prototype this before committing; it is the riskiest part of the hybrid.
- Per-topic prerequisite view (rec 9): raise from 'later, if wanted' to a serious candidate for the tapped-topic detail. The one open-learner-model study found rates a prerequisites view highest (4.4/5) and a colour grid mid (3.8/5) for 'what to work on next', which is exactly the map's gap-picking job. The grid stays as the overview; the 8-node per-topic layered graph (max 6 per layer, 59.7 pt pitch) would replace in-grid arrows.
- Fill-in staging (rec 5): size it for 1-3 changed tiles, not many. With the demo clock (box 1 due after 60 s, box 2 after 3 min) a 3-minute demo yields about 4-5 seen, 1-2 checked, at most 1-2 learned and zero retained. Drop the 'more than 5 changed, animate by row' rule; auto-focus the changed tile, and make seen and checked clearly visible fills rather than a small dot, or pre-seed a demo profile so the map is not 35 of 40 empty outlines.
- State encoding (rec 4): the 'retained' level (sky-100 + check) cannot appear in the 3-minute demo unless state is seeded; decide whether to seed. Keep the rest.
- Reduce Motion path (rec 6): keep, and add that useReducedMotion is read once at app start (not reactive), and check the demo phone's settings before presenting. React Native docs state Android's developer option 'Animation off' reports as reduce motion, which would silently turn the key beat into a snap.
- Android sizing (rec 1): the 'wrap below 380' rule does not reach Material's 48 dp on any phone up to 412 dp. Either accept about 45-47.5 dp as a documented exception (WCAG 24 px is met), or use 12 dp margins at 412 dp and wrap to 2 x 4 on everything narrower.
- Related chips (rec 2): de-duplicate RELATED_TO to 11 pairs when building chips or lines; quote 49 distinct edges, not 60.
- Later graph tooling (rec 9): if d3-dag is used, the current version is 1.2.2, not 1.1.0.

## Fact-check: what the track missed

- Liquid Glass on iOS 26: partial-height native sheets are glass by default, which clashes with the opaque dark theme; the track never checked iOS 26 behaviour of the sheet it recommends.
- Demo-clock arithmetic from docs/PLAN.md (lines 172-181): what the map can actually show after 3 minutes (about 5 of 40 tiles touched, none retained). The 'map visibly fills in' beat depends on this more than on animation timing.
- Edge routing inside a single-row-per-topic grid: collinear edges through intermediate tiles (28 of 35), and horizontal position not meaning the same depth in different rows.
- Vertical space budget for the map screen (status bar, header, Map|List control, counter, 5 labelled rows, panel) was never added up.
- Open-learner-model evidence (CEUR Vol-1618 LBR4) on which views learners find useful for choosing what to work on next; the PDF was already sitting in the session scratchpad.
- RELATED_TO is stored as symmetric directed records, so every related count in the track is doubled.
- @expo/ui (in Expo Go on SDK 57) has a SwiftUI BottomSheet and Compose ModalBottomSheet as another sheet route; props and Liquid Glass behaviour not checked by me either, so it is an option to test, not a recommendation.
- Screen-reader behaviour of a route-based sheet over the grid (VoiceOver focus confinement while the sheet is up, focus return on dismiss) is untested; an in-screen panel avoids the question.
- PLAN.md M6 acceptance says the heatmap 'updates immediately after an answer' and the smoke test asserts the level 'becomes learned'; the replay-on-open design needs a persisted last-seen snapshot that is separate from the real mastery state so those tests still pass.
- Not verified by me: Zong et al., W3C complex-images and HIG Charting quotes; Android behaviour of an undimmed formSheet (whether touches reach the grid).

## Findings

### 1. Our graph is five near-trees with few cross-topic prerequisites, and a global layered (Sugiyama-style) drawing cannot fit a phone at touch size, whereas an 8-per-row tile grid fits almost exactly.

Computed from graph.json: 40 concepts, 38 PREREQUISITE_OF edges (only 3 cross topics), 22 RELATED_TO edges (18 cross topics). Longest-path layers 0-4 have widths 6/20/7/5/2; in-degree is at most 2, out-degree at most 6. Per topic the widest layer is 6 (React Native, Problem solving). Selecting one node involves on average 2.45 direct edges (max 7) versus 60 in total; max 7 ancestors, 10 descendants. Titles: median 16 characters, max 36, only 11 of 40 are 12 characters or fewer. Geometry on 390 pt with 16 pt margins: 20-wide layer = 17.9 pt pitch; 8 columns = 44.75 pt; 6-wide per-topic layer = 59.7 pt; 5 columns = 71.6 pt. On a 360 dp Android phone 8 columns = 41 dp. docs/PLAN.md line 144 already specifies 'heatmap (topic rows, concept cells; unseen / seen / checked / learned / retained, plus a due marker)'.

Source: <file://content/software-engineering/graph.json> · confidence: high

### 2. Platform touch-target minimums: Apple 44 x 44 pt default (28 x 28 pt absolute minimum), Android/Material 48 x 48 dp with 8 dp spacing, WCAG 2.2 AA 24 x 24 CSS px (AAA 44 x 44), usability research about 1 cm.

Apple HIG Accessibility (JSON endpoint of the page): iOS default control size 44x44 pt, minimum 28x28 pt; 'about 12 points of padding around elements that include a bezel ... about 24 points' without a bezel. Google Android Accessibility Help (https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858): 48x48dp 'results in a physical size of about 9mm', recommended 7-10mm, targets 'separated by 8dp of space or more'. WCAG 2.5.8 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html): 'at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels', or a 24 px circle spacing exception. WCAG 2.5.5 AAA: 44 by 44. NN/g (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/): minimum 1cm x 1cm, citing Parhi, Karlson and Bederson. m3.material.io itself would not render, so the Material numbers come from Google's Android help page.

Source: <https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility.json> · confidence: high

### 3. Node-link diagrams stop being the best representation above roughly 20 nodes for most tasks; their one consistent advantage is path finding, and hairballs need interaction or filtering.

Ghoniem, Fekete and Castagliola (2005) abstract, read via OpenAlex: 'when graphs are bigger than twenty vertices, the matrix-based visualization outperforms node-link diagrams on most tasks. Only path finding is consistently in favor of node-link diagrams'. Yoghourdjian et al. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07944), 25-175 nodes: 'significant difficulty finding the shortest path in high-density node-link diagrams with more than 50 nodes and even low-density graphs with more than 100 nodes'; complex graphs require 'simplification through aggregation or interaction (such as filtering)'. Both studies used desktop screens; our 40 nodes at 7.7% density are inside the workable range on a monitor, but a phone has far less area. The only path task our learners have is 'what do I need before X', which a prerequisite chain answers.

Source: <https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1057/palgrave.ivs.9500092?select=title,publication_year,abstract_inverted_index> · confidence: medium

### 4. Radial tree layouts are measurably worse than top-down or orthogonal ones, which argues against sunburst, radial or constellation layouts for the prerequisite structure.

Burch et al. 2011 eye-tracking study abstract, read via OpenAlex: 'traditional and orthogonal tree diagrams significantly outperform the radial' layout on a least-common-ancestor task, and 'participants cross-checked their task solution more often in the radial than in the traditional and orthogonal layouts'. Desktop study, hierarchy task; applicability to a phone is an extrapolation.

Source: <https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/TVCG.2011.193?select=title,publication_year,abstract_inverted_index> · confidence: medium

### 5. Drawing cross-links straight on top of a grouped layout causes clutter; edge bundling reduces it but introduces ambiguity about which nodes are connected, and showing edges only for a selected node is an established alternative.

Holten 2006 (text extracted from the PDF): adding adjacency edges 'in a straightforward way ... generally leads to visual clutter'; bundling 'reduces visual clutter when dealing with large numbers of adjacency edges', but a bundle 'might contain each edge depicted' (bundling ambiguity), tuned with strength beta, e.g. 0.8; H3 'supports showing or hiding adjacency edges ... for a selected node or subtree'. Bundling targets hundreds of edges; we have 60, and focus-only display cuts that to a mean of 2.45 and a max of 7 per selection, so bundling is unnecessary.

Source: <https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~misha/ReadingSeminar/Papers/Holten06.pdf> · confidence: high

### 6. For small screens, evidence favours an overview that fits without panning plus discrete detail levels (semantic zoom) over free pan/zoom or fisheye distortion; on phones, small multiples beat animation for comparison speed.

Cockburn, Karlson and Bederson review (text extracted from the PDF): Pad introduced 'semantic zooming, which allows objects to be represented differently at different scales'; Hornbaek et al. 2002: an added overview 'increased task completion times when semantic zooming was enabled' because the overview became redundant; zooming's temporal separation meant 'participants had to reorient themselves with each zoom action. Animation can ease this problem'; Gutwin and Fedak 2004 on mobile devices: 'Panning slowest for all' tasks; fisheye menus 'inferior to traditional hierarchical menus'. Brehmer et al. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03919), 96 participants on their own phones: small multiples users 'consistently completed tasks in less time, albeit with slightly less confidence than those using an animated design'.

Source: <https://www.csse.canterbury.ac.nz/~andy/papers/fc.pdf> · confidence: medium

### 7. The best-known consumer learning app abandoned a navigable skill tree for a single guided path, because learners were unsure how to use the tree and review was not built in.

Duolingo's blog post on the home-screen redesign: learners said 'they're not sure whether they're using Duolingo the "correct" or "best" way'; the path builds in spacing ('it's more effective to space out practice for a particular concept than to cram') so users are 'regularly revisiting material'. No usage numbers are given. Implication for us: the feed agent already chooses the route, so the map's job is reflection and gap-picking, not route planning through a graph.

Source: <https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/> · confidence: medium

### 8. WCAG 2.2 requires 3:1 contrast for the parts of a graphic needed to understand it and forbids colour as the only cue; on our dark background a lightness ramp alone cannot make five states mutually distinguishable at 3:1.

SC 1.4.11: 'contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against adjacent color(s)' for 'Parts of graphics required to understand the content'; exception when 'The information is available in another form'. SC 1.4.1 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html): 'Color is not used as the only visual means of conveying information'; technique G111 is colour plus pattern. Computed: white on #0B1220 is 18.72:1 and on #13213D 16.0:1, so at most 2 levels can each be 3:1 from the background and from each other (3:1, 9:1; the next would need 27:1). A candidate ramp sky-600 #0284C7 / sky-500 #0EA5E9 / sky-300 #7DD3FC / sky-100 #E0F2FE is 3.9-16.3:1 against the background, but adjacent steps are only 1.45-1.7:1 apart. Outlines need relative luminance of at least 0.147 to reach 3:1 on #13213D: slate-500 #64748B passes (3.36:1), slate-600 #475569 fails (2.11:1).

Source: <https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html> · confidence: high

### 9. Keep ordered colour steps to 5-7, make the lightest colour mean 'most' on a dark theme, and stay on the blue-yellow axis; in the current reel palette sky and amber are colour-blind-safe but equal in luminance, and pink is not distinguishable from muted grey for red-green colour-blind viewers.

ColorBrewer: cartographers suggest five to seven classes; 'more classes require more colors that become increasingly difficult to tell apart'. Carbon data-viz palettes (https://carbondesignsystem.com/data-visualization/color-palettes/): 'In dark themes, the lightest color denotes the largest values.' Cividis paper (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0199239): CVD-optimised map 'varying straight from blue to yellow', brightness 'increases linearly'. Computed with the WCAG formula: sky #7DD3FC and amber #FBBF24 are both about 11.2:1 on #0B1220 and 1.00:1 against each other; pink #F472B6 is 6.0-7.1:1. Simulated with Machado et al. severity-1.0 matrices (https://www.inf.ufrgs.br/~oliveira/pubs_files/CVD_Simulation/CVD_Simulation.html, assumed linear RGB): sky vs amber stays far apart (delta-E about 104-107 protan/deutan), but pink vs muted #9AA7BD drops to delta-E 7 (deutan) and 13.5 (protan), and sky vs pink to about 24-27.

Source: <https://colorbrewer2.org/learnmore/number.html> · confidence: medium

### 10. Animated transitions beat static changes for tracking what changed, simple staging is preferred, and over-staging hurts; the research-backed pace is about 1 s per stage when things move, faster when movement is minimal.

Heer and Robertson 2007 (text extracted from the PDF): 'Animation is significantly better than static across all conditions'; 'staged animation was significantly preferred to animation (p < 0.003 in all cases)' except two heavily staged conditions where 'heavily staged animation resulted in increased error'; 'we recommend the use of simple staging'. Experiments used 1.25 s and 2 s transitions; 'recommend timing each stage around a full second, rather than around a half-second each'; 'transition times around 1 second, though transitions with minimal movement can likely be performed faster'. Principles: group similar transitions (common fate), minimise occlusion, slow-in slow-out, 'Make transitions as long as needed, but no longer'.

Source: <https://idl.cs.washington.edu/files/2007-AnimatedTransitions-InfoVis.pdf> · confidence: high

### 11. UI guidance puts single-element animations at 100-500 ms and reserves up to 1000 ms for large-area transitions; a change that happens off-screen or away from the user's focus goes unnoticed unless it is replayed, isolated and the rest dimmed.

NN/g: 'the duration of most animations should be in the range of 100-500 ms'; simple feedback 'roughly 100 ms'; substantial screen changes 200-300 ms; 'At 500ms, animations start to feel like a real drag'. Material motion tokens (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-components-android/master/docs/theming/Motion.md): short 50-200 ms, medium 250-400, long 450-600, extra-long 700-1000; standard easing cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1); 'duration should increase as the area/traversal of an animation increases'. NN/g change blindness (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/change-blindness-definition/): people 'ignore changes in a scene when they occur in a region that is far away from their focus of attention'; remedies: one change at a time, group simultaneous changes, dim unchanged areas. Our mastery changes happen in the feed while the map is not on screen, which is the worst case.

Source: <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/> · confidence: high

### 12. Motion must degrade gracefully: Reanimated skips animations by default when the OS Reduce Motion setting is on, Apple asks for fades instead of movement, and WCAG requires a pause control for auto-starting motion longer than 5 seconds.

Reanimated accessibility guide: 'By default all animations are configured with ReduceMotion.System'; when reduced motion is enabled, timing and spring animations 'return the toValue immediately', repeats do not start or run once; options ReduceMotion.System / Always / Never and a useReducedMotion hook. Apple HIG Accessibility: reduce 'zooming, scaling, and peripheral motion'; 'Replacing transitions in x-, y-, and z-axes with fades'. WCAG 2.2.2 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html): moving or blinking content that 'starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds, and is presented in parallel with other content' needs a way to pause, stop or hide it. So the fill-in animation silently becomes a snap under Reduce Motion, and an endlessly pulsing 'due' marker would breach 2.2.2.

Source: <https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-reanimated/docs/guides/accessibility/> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 13. Screen-reader users need a navigable structure, not just alt text; in React Native that means real accessible views per concept, and a Skia canvas does not provide them without overlays.

Zong et al. (MIT, https://vis.csail.mit.edu/pubs/rich-screen-reader-vis-experiences/): alt text plus data tables prevent exploration; design dimensions are structure, navigation (structural, spatial, targeted) and description; users want 'overview first, zoom and filter, and details on demand'. W3C WAI complex images (https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/): short description plus a long description available to everyone. Apple HIG Charting data: 'Make every chart in your app accessible' and 'Keep a chart simple, letting people choose when they want additional details'. React Native docs (https://reactnative.dev/docs/accessibility): accessibilityRole, accessibilityState {disabled, selected, checked, busy, expanded}, accessibilityValue {min, max, now, text}, accessibilityElementsHidden / importantForAccessibility='no-hide-descendants'. React Native Skia docs: Canvas takes View accessibility props, but individual drawn elements need Views overlaid on the canvas. Expo SDK 57 docs list both react-native-svg and @shopify/react-native-skia as 'Included in Expo Go' (https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/svg/, .../sdk/skia/).

Source: <https://wcandillon.github.io/react-native-skia/docs/canvas/overview> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 14. A half-height sheet is the platform-standard way to show detail while keeping the map visible, tap should be the only required gesture, and Expo Router exposes the needed sheet options.

Apple HIG Sheets: medium detent is 'About half of the fully expanded height'; 'consider supporting the medium detent to allow progressive disclosure'; include a grabber, which 'also works with VoiceOver'; sheets can be nonmodal so people keep using the parent view. HIG Gestures (https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures.json): tap = 'Activate a control; select an item', touch and hold = 'Reveal additional controls or functionality'; 'Use shortcut gestures to supplement standard gestures, not replace them'; HIG Accessibility: 'avoid custom multifinger and multihand gestures'. Expo Router modals docs (https://docs.expo.dev/router/advanced/modals/, unversioned page): presentation 'formSheet', sheetAllowedDetents as fractions (e.g. [0.25, 0.5, 1]) or 'fitToContents', sheetInitialDetentIndex, sheetGrabberVisible (iOS only), sheetLargestUndimmedDetentIndex; 'Android supports a maximum of 3 detents'. Half of 844 pt is 422 pt; the 5 x 8 grid with row labels needs about 320-340 pt, so it stays visible above the sheet.

Source: <https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets.json> · confidence: medium · version-sensitive

## Open questions

- No primary study was found that tests node-link DAGs versus grids on phone-sized touchscreens for learners; the layout recommendation rests on geometry plus desktop graph-readability and mobile small-multiples studies. A 10-minute paper or Expo Go prototype test of grid-with-focus-edges versus a per-topic graph would settle it.
- Is the demo iPhone-only? On a 360 dp Android phone the 8-column grid gives 41 dp tiles (under Material's 48 dp); the 2 x 4 wrap per topic fixes it but changes the look.
- Should 'seen' appear on a mastery map at all? It reflects exposure, not retrieval, and could dilute the 'only retrieval counts' message; a faint dot is the compromise proposed, but it is a product call.
- Should box 3 and box 4 (both 'retained') look different in the overview, or only in the sheet? The task brief mentions six ordered states while docs/PLAN.md lists five.
- For the 3-minute demo, is opening the map the right moment for the fill-in animation, or should a small in-feed mini-map flash after a correct delayed answer so the beat does not depend on navigation? Not tested.
- Whether dashed RELATED_TO lines on focus stay readable when 18 of 22 cross topic rows, or whether chips in the sheet are enough, needs a quick visual prototype.
- Open-learner-model literature (Bull and Kay; a LEA's Box study reportedly logging skill meters and treemaps as the most-used views) could not be opened (Springer login redirect, certificate error), so that evidence is not included. Purchase 1997 on edge crossings was also only available as a search summary.
- The Expo Router sheet options were read from the unversioned docs page, not a v57-specific page; confirm sheetLargestUndimmedDetentIndex and fractional detents behave as documented in Expo Go on SDK 57 before relying on a non-dimmed, interactive background.
- The colour-blindness check used Machado et al. matrices applied in linear RGB with a simple CIE76 delta-E; the source page does not state the colour space, so treat the delta-E figures as indicative and re-check the final palette with a simulator on device.
