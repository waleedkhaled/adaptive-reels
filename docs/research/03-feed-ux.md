# UX of a short-video learning feed with in-feed retrieval

Keep the TikTok grammar for navigation (vertical swipe, tap to pause, loop, no auto-advance) but change three things for learning: make the "why this reel" reason and the three quick actions always visible instead of buried in menus, treat watching as "Seen" only, and prove mastery with interleaved multiple-choice checks that come after a lag and always show the answer plus a short explanation. The evidence is consistent: re-watching wins at 5 minutes and loses at 1 week (Roediger & Karpicke), polished video inflates the feeling of learning (Carpenter; Deslauriers), interleaved tests halve mind wandering (Szpunar), longer lags make retrieval harder but nearly double later recall (Pyc & Rawson), and feedback matters even after correct answers (Butler et al.). Mainstream feeds hide explanations two taps deep and their "not interested" controls often do little (Mozilla: 11% effective), so our controls must change the very next item and say so. One repo-specific constraint drives the layout: captions are burned into the 720x1280 reels 120-150 px from the bottom with 48 px side padding, so a TikTok-style right rail or a "cover" fit would collide with or crop text; use "contain" and put chrome in the letterbox on tall phones. Not verified from primary sources: tap-to-pause and double-tap-like conventions, Kizilcec 2016, the full attention-capture typology, Little et al. 2012, and Quizlet/Imprint/Headway patterns (pages blocked).

## Recommendations

- **must**: Player layout: use contentFit 'contain', top-align the video under the status bar, and put chrome outside the picture on tall phones: a bottom bar in the letterbox with a thin progress line and the three quick actions as labelled buttons (icon + text, at least 44 pt). Put the reason chip over the top band of the video. Do not use a TikTok-style right rail and never use 'cover'. When re-rendering, reserve a text-free top band (about 110 px of 1280) and raise captions to leave a text-free bottom band (about 200 px) so 16:9 phones, which have no letterbox, can overlay the same bar. _Why:_ Captions are burned in 120-150 px from the bottom with 48 px side padding; cover crops about 65 px per side on a 393x852 phone and a right rail or bottom overlay would cover caption words. Contain leaves about 60 pt of safe free space on current iPhones and more on 20:9 Android. Re-rendering costs no API money.
- **must**: Keep feed gestures conventional but never gesture-only: swipe up/down for next/previous, single tap toggles pause with a visible paused glyph, the reel loops and never auto-advances, double-tap triggers 'More like this' with a visible confirmation. Do not hide any essential action behind long-press. Give every gesture a visible button and a VoiceOver action, and show a persistent mute toggle whose state carries across reels. _Why:_ TikTok hides 'Not interested' behind a long-press and the reason behind the share panel; Mozilla shows users distrust controls they cannot see working. WCAG 2.5.1 and Apple HIG require alternatives to gestures; WCAG 1.4.2 and HIG require control over autoplaying audio; Lukoff et al. found autoplay undermines sense of agency. Tap-to-pause and double-tap conventions were not verifiable from official docs, so test them with the three testers.
- **must**: Retrieval card anatomy: a full-screen item in the same pager, headed by its reason (for example 'Check: {concept}, seen 3 reels ago' or 'Review: learned 2 min ago'); one question, 3-4 full-width options; no timer and no auto-advance. On answer: lock the options, mark the correct one with an icon and a text label, show the 1-2 sentence explanation for both correct and wrong answers, then a Continue button (swipe also works). After a wrong answer say that another angle comes next, play the reteach reel, and re-check later in the same session. _Why:_ Feedback lifts 1-week recall from .31 to .49-.56 and stops wrong options being learned (Butler & Roediger 2008); feedback after a correct guess keeps .85 vs .40 (Butler, Karpicke & Roediger 2008); explanations beat right/wrong only (Ryan 2020). Wrong-then-retest-after-other-items is the successive relearning procedure. WCAG 2.2.1 rules out time limits; 1.4.1 rules out colour-only marking.
- **must**: Timing and honesty of mastery: keep cards interleaved (Light every 4 reels, Focus every 2), never place a concept's first check directly after its reel, and raise the minimum gap in PLAN.md from 1 to 2 intervening items (about 60 s). Keep 'learned' = a correct answer after the due time. Label states by evidence everywhere (Seen = watched, Checked = answered once, Learned = recalled after a delay) and show both counts on the map header, for example 'Watched 12, proven 3'. _Why:_ Interleaved tests cut mind wandering from 41% to 19% and raised later learning (Szpunar 2013); a 34-item lag gave .54 final recall vs .30 for a 6-item lag (Pyc & Rawson 2009); immediate performance overstates learning and learners cannot feel the difference (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Carpenter; Deslauriers). The demo clock (60 s for box 1) already satisfies the gap.
- **must**: Reason chip: always visible on every reel and card, one line of roughly 40 characters, rendered from the agent's reason code with the concrete cause (for example 'Review: you learned {concept} 2 min ago', 'Because you tapped More like this', 'Unlocked by {prereq}'). Pair the pool colour with an icon or word. Tapping opens a small sheet with 2-3 lines of detail and the matching control (less of this topic, more like this). Never show a reason the agent did not actually use. _Why:_ Mainstream apps bury generic reasons two taps deep; Springer & Whittaker found simple-first with detail on demand works better than continuous detail; Tintarev & Masthoff show persuasive explanations can mislead. A rule-based agent can be exactly truthful, which is the trust advantage. The demo needs the adaptation to be visible without a tap.
- **must**: Quick actions must visibly change the next item: after a tap show an inline confirmation that states the consequence with Undo (for example 'Less {topic} this session, Undo'); the next reel's chip cites the action. 'Too easy' immediately announces 'Prove it: 1 question'. Say that 'Not for me' is session-scoped. Log taps and undos. _Why:_ Mozilla found 'Not interested' prevented only 11% of unwanted recommendations and 39% of users felt controls did nothing; Instagram and Spotify scope hide/snooze in time (30 days), which makes negative feedback low-risk. PLAN.md already re-plans the very next slot after a quick action, so the UI only needs to surface it.
- **should**: Onboarding in one screen and under 15 seconds: all 5 topics as large chips, pick any number including none, a Skip button of equal weight, no account. Add one sentence that sets the contract: watching marks a concept Seen; only answering a check after a delay marks it Learned; your swipes and the three buttons steer the feed. If skipped, start all topics at a neutral prior instead of 0.3. _Why:_ TikTok and Netflix both make the picker optional and say behaviour quickly overrides it; Deslauriers et al. found learners rate effortful active learning lower unless the reason is explained early, and Higham et al. found initial anxiety about testing reverses with experience. A neutral prior avoids penalising all topics when the user skips.
- **should**: Design the session to end and remove pressure mechanics: no auto-advance; when due reviews are cleared or after about 10 items in Light mode insert a summary card (concepts proven this session, next review due in X) that needs an explicit 'Keep going' tap. No streaks, no loss-framed copy, no notifications in the demo. Let the learner swipe past a card as 'Later' with no mastery change (it returns). Count at most one rewatch per impression and pause after two idle loops. _Why:_ Lukoff et al. on agency; Instagram's caught-up marker and TikTok's active-decision prompt are the established stopping patterns; Duolingo states streaks run on loss aversion and that breaking one is demotivating; forced reactions improved recall but frustrated most users (N=30). An auto-looping idle reel would otherwise add +0.10 interest per loop under the current signal table.
- **should**: Haptics and sound: selection haptic when an option is pressed, success notification on a correct answer and when a map cell is promoted, one soft impact for a wrong answer, nothing on swipes. Add a settings toggle and never rely on haptics alone. Skip sound effects in the demo; if added later they must follow the mute toggle and not overlap narration. _Why:_ Apple HIG: use system haptics by their meaning, consistently and sparingly, and make them optional. expo-haptics does nothing in Low Power Mode or when the user has disabled it, so every haptic needs a visual twin. The reels already carry narration audio.
- **should**: Accessibility baseline: build cards and chrome with React Native Text and font scaling on; let card content scroll at 200% and cap only chrome with maxFontSizeMultiplier (about 1.4). When Reduce Motion is on, replace the pager slide, the map fill and any celebration with a cross-fade. Encode correct/wrong, pools and map states with icon or shape plus a label, never amber vs sky alone. Expose each reel's transcript (manifest captions) as the video's accessibility label and add VoiceOver actions for next, previous and the three quick actions. Keep all targets at least 44 pt. _Why:_ WCAG 2.2 (1.4.1, 1.4.4, 2.5.1, 2.2.2) and Apple HIG accessibility. Burned-in captions cannot be read by a screen reader or scaled, but the manifest already carries the words. Amber #FBBF24 and sky #7DD3FC have a 1.0:1 luminance ratio, so they are indistinguishable without hue; all palette colours otherwise pass 4.5:1 on both background stops.

## Fact-check: changes to the recommendations

- Top band: do not overlay the reason chip on the video's top band as rendered today; it collides with the burned-in '{topic} · {title}' chip (52-103 px) and progress bar. Either re-render with the Chrome component removed and draw topic, reason and progress natively (accessible, scalable), or keep the burned-in chrome and skip the app progress line.
- Drop 're-render with a 110 px top band and a 200 px bottom band' for 16:9 phones. Raising captions to 200 px puts the caption block at about y=970, directly under on-screen text (visual-focus: text from 800 to about 960) and the 400-960 visual box, so it needs a layout redesign and a visual re-check of 119 reels. Cheaper: on phones with no letterbox, shrink the video to fit above a 56 pt bar (about 92% scale on 375x667, 15 pt pillars).
- Do not raise the minimum gap from 1 to 2 intervening items under the demo clock. Pyc & Rawson does not support it, and it breaks the 180 s acceptance test: Focus with gap 1 reaches 'learned' at about 140-150 s; with gap 2 at about 175-180 s with no slack (before 15 s onboarding). Apply a longer or time-based gap (60 s or more) only on the real clock.
- Light mode cannot close the demo loop: a card every 4 reels of 30 s puts the first check at about 120 s and the review at 190 s or later. The demo must start in Focus, or the demo clock must also set cadence to 2.
- Sound default is a decision, not an open question: on iOS expo-video plays through the silent switch. Start muted with a visible 'Tap for sound' (burned-in captions carry the lesson) or ask once in onboarding; persist the choice; set nativeControls={false}; note that unmuted playback with audioMixingMode 'auto' stops the learner's music.
- Reduce Motion: keep the finger-tracked swipe between items (HIG lists gesture-tracked animation as acceptable); cross-fade only programmatic transitions (Continue auto-advance, map fill, celebration).
- Retrieval card: 'content scrolls at 200%' plus 'swipe also continues' plus 'swipe past = Later' conflict inside a vertical pager, and a habitual swipe would silently skip checks (a watch-only session, and a failed demo loop). Lock the pager on an unanswered card; offer an explicit 'Later' button (logged) and a Continue button; allow swipe only after the answer.
- Fix reason and confirmation copy to match the evidence labels: REVIEW_DUE at box 1 should say 'you answered this {elapsed} ago', not 'you learned'; the 'Not for me' toast should say the concept is hidden this session and the topic will show less (that part persists), not 'this session' for everything.
- 'Never place a first check directly after its reel' contradicts PLAN's immediate prove-it card after 'Too easy'. Make the exception explicit: a prove-it answer can only reach Checked, and should use an item not answerable from the reel just watched (the pitfall item).
- Prefer 3 options over 4 on first checks and always rotate items: lure intrusions grow with option count and with little prior study (one 30 s reel is little study). Use a different retrieval item and shuffled options for the delayed review so 'Learned' is not recognition of the same card.
- Targets and haptics per platform: 48 dp minimum on Android (44 pt is the iOS figure); on Android call performAndroidHapticsAsync (Confirm / Reject) instead of notificationAsync, which is a Vibrator simulation there.
- Drop double-tap for 'More like this' in the demo: the labelled button already exists, the convention was not verified, and recognising a double tap delays every single-tap pause.
- Positioning: stop calling in-feed quizzes the differentiator; ScrollEd and 5Mins.ai already put quizzes in a swipe feed. Lead with delayed-retrieval-gated mastery, the visible truthful reason, and the map; look at those two apps before finalising the card pattern.
- Soften the 'fluent video inflates the feeling of learning' wording in onboarding copy and rationale: the 22-minute replication did not find overall confidence inflation. The watched-is-not-learned rule still stands on Roediger & Karpicke and Deslauriers.

## Fact-check: what the track missed

- The reels already contain burned-in chrome (top progress bar and topic/title chip); the track designed app chrome as if the frame were clean.
- No place for navigation: the 60 pt safe letterbox on a 393x852 iPhone fits one 44 pt row only, so quick actions and a tab bar cannot both live there. Where the knowledge-map entry and the Light/Focus toggle go was not addressed (for example a top-right button). On Android, 3-button navigation eats part of the band.
- Demo time budget was never checked against the recommendations: 15 s onboarding, card cadence, minimum gap, 60 s box-1 interval and reading time all draw on the same 180 s.
- expo-video defaults that shape the UX: nativeControls true, timeUpdateEventInterval 0, audio session always .playback on iOS, 'auto' mixing interrupts other audio, documented 'cover' overlap bug.
- Gesture conflicts: vertical scroll inside a vertical pager on cards; single tap vs double tap; accidental swipe past a card.
- Item reuse: nothing on rotating the 3 retrieval items per concept or shuffling options between first check and delayed review, which decides whether 'Learned' means recall or card recognition.
- Lure learning on early multiple-choice checks (Butler & Roediger): worse with little prior study and more options; bears directly on option count and on first checks after a single reel.
- Direct competitors with quizzes in a swipe feed (ScrollEd, 5Mins.ai) were not found; Quizlet, Imprint and Headway were left unopened.
- Android specifics for the first tester build: 48 dp targets, Vibrator-simulated haptics, system back gesture on cards and sheets.
- Re-render cost is not only compute: PLAN allows only human-approved reels, so any layout change (caption or band moves) implies a visual re-check of 119 reels; removing chrome alone is low risk.
- All learning evidence is from students with prose, word pairs or lectures; none tests 30 s reels or working developers, and the platform UI sources are from 2019-2023. The track flagged the first only partly.

## Findings

### 1. Re-watching looks better than testing after 5 minutes but loses clearly after 2 days and 1 week, and learners predict the opposite. A watch-only signal therefore cannot stand for mastery, and an immediate check overstates it.

Roediger & Karpicke 2006, Exp 1: recall at 5 min was 81% after restudy vs 75% after a test; at 2 days 54% vs 68%; at 1 week 42% vs 56%. Exp 2 at 1 week: one study + three tests 61%, three studies + one test 56%, four studies 40%, although the study-only group read the passage 14.2 times vs 3.4. Self-rated 'how well will I remember' was highest in the study-only group (4.8 vs 4.0 on a 7-point scale). No feedback was given on the tests.

Source: <https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2006-roediger.pdf> · confidence: high

### 2. Fluent, polished video raises the feeling of learning without raising learning, and the evidence for short-form video as a learning format is immediate-test only. Our reels (TTS, kinetic text, 30 s) are exactly the fluent kind, so the UI must not let 'watched' read as 'learned'.

Carpenter et al. 2016 (describing Carpenter et al. 2013): same scripted content delivered fluently vs haltingly got instructor ratings of 4.2 vs 1.5 out of 5 and higher predicted test scores, with no significant difference in actual learning. Deslauriers et al. 2019 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6765278/): passive lectures produced a higher feeling of learning (about 0.56 SD) but lower test scores (about 0.46 SD) than active sessions; explaining this to students early improved attitudes. 'The Reel Deal' (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05962v1, N=62): 30-60 s reels beat long video on an immediate quiz (93.9% vs 79.7%), but the authors state long-term retention and novelty effects are untested. A 2025 Psychological Bulletin meta-analysis (71 studies, 98,299 participants; summary opened at https://www.psypost.org/large-meta-analysis-links-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-to-poorer-cognitive-and-mental-health/) links heavier short-form video use to poorer attention and inhibitory control; mostly cross-sectional, so not causal.

Source: <https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10016564> · confidence: high

### 3. Interleaving short tests between video segments (rather than one quiz at the end) roughly halves mind wandering and improves learning of the NEXT segment; mere re-exposure to the answers does not. This supports cards inside the feed every few reels.

Szpunar, Khan & Schacter 2013, Exp 2 (21-min lecture in four ~5.5-min segments, six 20-s questions after each): mind wandering on probes 19% (tested) vs 39% (restudy of question+answer) vs 41% (not tested); notes on 24% vs 9% vs 7% of slides; score on the final segment 89% vs 65% vs 70%. Exp 1: 84% vs 59%. Tested students also reported less anxiety about the final cumulative test and lower mental demand.

Source: <https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/f3608457-a87d-4171-8f75-b068482e8233/content> · confidence: high

### 4. A check placed after a longer lag is harder but much more effective than one placed right after exposure, and repeated correct answers within one sitting show diminishing returns. Mastery should only be credited after a delay.

Pyc & Rawson 2009, Exp 1: with 6 intervening items (about 1 min) between retrievals, final recall averaged .30; with 34 intervening items (about 6 min) it averaged .54. Benefit per extra correct retrieval in the same session shrank as the count rose. Bjork & Bjork 2023 (https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-introducing-desirable-difficulties-into-practice-and-instruction-bjork-and-bjork.pdf) state that "current performance is not a reliable index of learning", which needs a delayed test, and that a difficulty stops being desirable when the learner lacks the background to succeed at it (supports prerequisite locking and reteach before re-check).

Source: <https://andymatuschak.org/prompts/Pyc2009.pdf> · confidence: high

### 5. Successive relearning is the model our Leitner boxes should imitate: within a session, a missed item is restudied and tested again after other items until it is answered correctly once; then it is relearned in at least two more spaced sessions. Relearning gets fast, which makes later reviews cheap.

Dunlosky, Greve, Badali, Wissman & Rawson 2023 (APA Div. 2 chapter): defines the procedure as above (correct = done for the session; incorrect = study the answer, re-test later in the same session). Rawson et al. 2013: about +10% on course exams, and 24 days later over 60% recalled vs under 20% for self-chosen study. Time per set drops from 30-40 min to about 15 min to under 5 min across sessions. Higham et al. 2022: learners first judged restudy as more effective and testing as more anxiety-provoking; both reversed after experiencing it.

Source: <https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-successive-relearning-dunlosky-greve-badali-wissman-rawson.pdf> · confidence: high

### 6. Multiple-choice checks need feedback every time: without it learners absorb the wrong options and become overconfident. Immediate per-item feedback is clearly better than none; feedback shown a little later was better still.

Butler & Roediger 2008: cued recall 1 week later was .56 with feedback after the whole test, .49 with feedback right after each item, .31 with no feedback, .14 with no test. Without feedback, lure answers intruded more as options increased (.19, .26, .29 for 2, 4, 6 options); with feedback intrusions stayed around .13-.17. Confidence matched accuracy in the feedback groups and was too high without feedback (.31 correct vs 39 confidence). 'Delayed' meant shortly after the test, not days later.

Source: <https://mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Formative%20Assessment/Feedback%20enhances%20the%20positive%20effects%20and%20reduces.pdf> · confidence: high

### 7. Feedback is needed after CORRECT answers too (a lucky guess is otherwise lost), and a short explanation beats a bare right/wrong mark. So the card should show the answer and explanation in both cases, which also makes a separate confidence step unnecessary for retention.

Butler, Karpicke & Roediger 2008: of answers that were correct but rated as guesses, .85 were retained with feedback vs .40 without; all initially correct answers .93 vs .79. Ryan et al. 2020 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7550480/): later transfer scores were 51.3% with right/wrong only, 56.3% with a brief why-each-option explanation, 59.4% with a concept-focused explanation.

Source: <https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2008/2008_Butler_Karpicke_Roediger_JEPLMC.pdf> · confidence: high

### 8. Learning products put the check inside the lesson with instant feedback and show mastery decaying; video feeds do not. No mainstream short-video feed has in-feed retrieval, so this is our differentiator and has no convention to copy.

Duolingo (2019 blog): on a recognised mistake it shows a short explanation plus a small follow-up exercise; mastered skills visibly 'break' when its spaced-repetition model says review is due; checkpoint quizzes end each section. Brilliant (https://brilliant.org/about/): one concept per lesson, asks before it teaches, instant answer-specific feedback. Khan Academy (https://blog.khanacademy.org/why-khan-academy-will-be-using-skills-to-proficient-to-measure-learning-outcomes/): levels attempted, familiar, proficient, mastered, moved only by assessed answers. YouTube's 2023 AI-quiz test (https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/07/youtube-tests-ai-generated-quizzes-on-educational-videos/) placed quizzes on the home feed under recently watched educational videos, not in the Shorts player. TikTok's STEM feed (https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/take-discovery-to-a-new-level-with-stem-feed) is a vetted content feed with no quiz mechanism. Quizlet, Imprint and Headway pages could not be opened.

Source: <https://blog.duolingo.com/how-weve-improved-the-duolingo-learning-experience-this-year-and-a-sneak-peek-toward-2020/> · confidence: medium · version-sensitive

### 9. In mainstream feeds the explanation and the negative-feedback control are hidden and generic, and independent measurement shows such controls often barely work, which users notice. Our advantage is a rule-based agent that can state the real reason and change the next item.

Mozilla (22,722 participants, YouTube): 'Not interested' prevented 11% of unwanted recommendations and 'Don't recommend channel' 43%; 62.3% of surveyed users said the controls did nothing or had mixed results (39.3% no impact). TikTok (https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/learn-why-a-video-is-recommended-for-you): the reason sits behind the share panel then a question-mark item and lists broad categories (your interactions, accounts you follow, recent or popular content in your region); 'Not interested' is behind a long-press (https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/limiting-unwanted-content). Instagram (https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained): reason behind the three-dot menu; offers Not interested, a tested Interested button, and a 30-day snooze of suggestions. Spotify (https://www.spotify.com/us/safetyandprivacy/understanding-recommendations/plain): thumbs-down, hide or 30-day snooze, exclude from taste profile.

Source: <https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/blog/mozilla-investigation-youtubes-dislike-button-other-user-controls-largely-fail-to-stop-unwanted-recommendations/> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 10. Explanation research favours a short, simple reason first with detail on demand, and warns that explanations tuned to please or persuade can make users' expectations less accurate. One line on the chip, more in a sheet, and only true reasons.

Springer & Whittaker (two studies): users expected to want detailed incremental feedback but, after use, found it distracting and it interfered with forming a working mental model; the authors recommend progressive disclosure starting from simplified feedback. Tintarev & Masthoff (slide summary opened at https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/748d/1e4d801d56a12c7494153ce22a5808fae404.pdf): seven aims (transparency, scrutability, trust, effectiveness, persuasiveness, efficiency, satisfaction) that trade off; feature-based explanations raised satisfaction while personalised ones reduced effectiveness. Kizilcec 2016 (moderate transparency raised trust, too much lowered it) is known only from search results; the ACM page returned 403.

Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02164> · confidence: medium

### 11. Onboarding interest pickers in the reference products are optional and explicitly low-stakes: behaviour overrides them quickly. With only 5 topics we can show all of them on one screen and allow skipping.

TikTok: new users are invited to select interest categories; if they decline they get a general feed of popular videos that is refined from their first interactions; finishing a longer video is weighted as a strong signal. Netflix (https://help.netflix.com/en/node/100639): choosing a few titles at sign-up is optional, skipping yields a diverse popular set, and the initial choices are soon superseded by viewing behaviour.

Source: <https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 12. Auto-advance and recommendation-driven feeds reduce users' sense of agency; forced interaction improves recall but frustrates; the accepted mitigations are natural stopping points and an explicit decision to continue. Streaks work by loss aversion, which Duolingo itself says is demotivating when the streak breaks.

Lukoff et al. 2021 (survey N=120, 13 co-design sessions): autoplay and recommendations mainly undermine sense of agency, search and playlists support it; users with a specific intention prefer higher-agency designs. Ruiz et al. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18803, N=30): requiring a reaction before the next post reduced mindless scrolling and improved content recall, but most participants found it frustrating. Instagram (https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/introducing-youre-all-caught-up-in-feed): a caught-up marker once all posts from the last two days are seen. TikTok (https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/new-features-for-teens-and-families-on-tiktok-us): at 60 minutes teens must enter a passcode, an active decision to continue. Duolingo (https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/): streaks rely on loss aversion; a broken streak is demotivating; a second streak freeze raised daily active learners by 0.38%. The CHI 2023 attention-capture typology (11 patterns incl. infinite scroll, never-ending autoplay) was confirmed only at abstract level.

Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11778> · confidence: medium

### 13. Our reels constrain the player layout: captions are burned in near the bottom with narrow side margins, and the video is 9:16 while most phones are about 19.5:9. 'cover' would crop text; a right-hand action rail or bottom overlay would sit on the captions; 'contain' leaves a free band on tall phones but none on 16:9 phones.

Repo, pipeline/src/render/remotion/Reel.tsx: captions are 42 px bold, two lines of up to 7 words, positioned 120-150 px above the bottom of the 1280 px frame with 48 px side padding; kinetic text uses 56 px side padding. Computed: on a 393x852 pt iPhone, contain gives a 699 pt tall video and 153 pt free (60 pt inside the safe areas); cover would crop about 65 px (of 720) per side, more than the text padding. On a 375x667 (16:9) phone there is no free band. On 412x915 Android about 135 pt is free. Captions scale to about 23 pt and sit 65-82 pt above the video's bottom edge. expo-video v57 docs: contentFit accepts contain, cover, fill; timeUpdate events with a configurable interval support a custom progress bar; muted and loop are player properties; the library runs in Expo Go.

Source: <https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/video/> · confidence: high · version-sensitive

### 14. Platform accessibility and haptics rules that bind this app: no colour-only states, no gesture-only functions, no time limits on cards, pause control for moving content, audio control for autoplaying sound, 44 pt targets, text that scales to 200%, fades when Reduce Motion is on, and sparse, optional haptics that can silently not fire. Our palette passes contrast, but amber and sky have identical luminance.

## Open questions

- Does expo-video on SDK 57 play narration when the iOS silent switch is on? The v57 docs do not say. Test on a phone, then decide the default mute state; the silent switch cannot be read in Expo Go.
- Confidence capture: is a one-tap 'Sure / Not sure' before the reveal worth the friction? Feedback already protects low-confidence correct answers (.85 vs .40), so it is not needed for retention; its only use would be to stop a guessed-correct answer promoting the Leitner box. No evidence found on the friction cost in a feed.
- Should cards offer an 'I don't know' option that counts as wrong and triggers the reteach reel? It would avoid forced guessing, but I found no direct evidence opened for or against.
- Minimum lag before the first check: evidence favours longer lags (34 vs 6 items), but success must stay high enough to be a desirable difficulty and the demo must close the loop in about 3 minutes. Log first-check accuracy by gap (scheduled_gap_ms vs actual_gap_ms already exist) and tune.
- Burned-in vs app-rendered captions: burned-in gives exact sync at zero runtime cost but cannot scale, hide or be read by VoiceOver. Rendering from manifest word timings needs frequent timeUpdate events; whether that holds about 100 ms sync in Expo Go on a mid-range phone is untested.
- 16:9 phones (for example iPhone SE) have no letterbox, so the action bar must overlay the video. Is re-rendering with reserved top and bottom bands acceptable for the reel design, or should small screens get a compact overlay variant?
- Should a retrieval card be skippable in Focus mode, or only in Light? The friction study (N=30) suggests forced interaction helps recall but frustrates; no evidence specific to learning feeds.
- Tap-to-pause and double-tap-to-like are treated as conventions; I could not open official TikTok, Instagram or YouTube documentation that states them (support pages are script-rendered or silent). Validate with the three testers.
- Not opened, so claims resting on them are unverified: Kizilcec 2016 (ACM 403), the full 11-pattern list in Monge Roffarello et al. 2023 (PDF too large), Little et al. 2012 on plausible distractors, Curious Shorts CHI 2025 (ACM 403), and Quizlet, Imprint and Headway product docs.
- Feedback timing: Butler & Roediger found feedback shortly after the whole test (.56) beat per-item immediate feedback (.49). In a feed each card is a single item, so does repeating the explanation at the next review or in the session summary capture that benefit? Untested.
